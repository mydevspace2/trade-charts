R-466A PT Chart

At 40 °F, R-466A has a dew pressure of 110.8 psig and a bubble pressure of 115.9 psig (temperature glide about 2.3 °F). At 100 °F the bubble (liquid) pressure is 308.6 psig. Use the lookup below for any temperature or pressure, or scroll to the full chart in °F and °C.

How to read this R-466A PT chart

R-466A is a zeotropic blend, so it does not boil at a single temperature for a given pressure. The bubble point is where the liquid starts to boil; the dew point is where the last drop of liquid has evaporated. The difference is the temperature glide. Inside the evaporator the refrigerant moves from bubble toward dew, so when you calculate superheat use the dew column; in the condenser it moves from dew to bubble, so for subcooling use the bubble column. Using the wrong column on a high-glide blend can throw your superheat or subcooling off by several degrees and lead to an over- or under-charge.

All pressures are gauge pressures at sea level (psig = psia − 14.7). At high altitude a gauge zeroed to the local atmosphere reads higher than the chart for the same temperature (roughly 0.5 psi per 1,000 ft); digital manifolds with an altitude setting correct for this. Values shown in inHg vac are vacuum (below atmospheric pressure). The chart describes saturated refrigerant only; it does not tell you whether a system is correctly charged by itself.

Worked example: superheat and subcooling with R-466A

Superheat. Suction pressure at the service valve reads 111 psig. On this chart that pressure is a dew temperature of about 40 °F. Your clamp thermometer on the suction line near the same point reads 52 °F, so superheat = 52 − 40 = 12 °F. Compare it with the target from the manufacturer’s charging chart or our target superheat calculator (fixed orifice systems).

Subcooling. Liquid line pressure reads 331 psig, which is a bubble temperature of about 105 °F. The liquid line measures 95 °F, so subcooling = 105 − 95 = 10 °F. TXV and EEV systems are normally charged to the subcooling printed on the outdoor unit’s rating plate. Do the math automatically with the superheat and subcooling calculators.

How this chart was made and checked

Pressures are computed with CoolProp 8, an open-source property library that implements the same Helmholtz equations of state used by NIST REFPROP, at 1 °F steps with the ASHRAE Standard 34 blend composition. We then checked the result against published charts: Honeywell Solstice N41 presentation (average pressure, bar) (3 points, max 0.12 °F). Across all 3 compared points the largest difference is 0.12 °F in saturation temperature (our pass limit is 1.0 °F, about the resolution of a good digital gauge). Points within 2 psi of atmospheric pressure are not compared because printed charts switch to vacuum units there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the R-466A pressure at 40 °F?
R-466A saturates at 110.8 psig (dew) and 115.9 psig (bubble) at 40 °F.
What is the R-466A pressure at 100 °F?
The bubble (liquid) saturation pressure of R-466A at 100 °F is 308.6 psig and the dew pressure is 297.6 psig.
Should I use bubble or dew for R-466A?
Use the dew point for superheat (evaporator and suction line) and the bubble point for subcooling (condenser and liquid line). For R-466A the glide at 40 °F is about 2.3 °F.
Can I top off a leaking R-466A system?
R-466A is a blend; a vapor leak can shift its composition. Manufacturers recommend charging blends as liquid and, after a large leak, recovering and recharging with new refrigerant. Follow the equipment manufacturer’s instructions.
Is R-466A flammable?
No. R-466A is ASHRAE safety group A1 (no flame propagation). It can still displace oxygen and form toxic products in a flame.
Where does this R-466A chart come from?
CoolProp 8, an open-source REFPROP-equivalent property library, checked against 3 points from published charts with a maximum difference of 0.12 °F. Sources are listed on the page.
