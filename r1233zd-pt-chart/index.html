How to read this R-1233zd(E) PT chart

R-1233zd(E) is a single-component refrigerant, so each temperature has exactly one saturation pressure. Find the measured pressure in the psig column and read the saturated temperature beside it (or type it into the lookup above). That saturated temperature is the starting point for both superheat (suction side) and subcooling (liquid side).

All pressures are gauge pressures at sea level (psig = psia − 14.7). At high altitude a gauge zeroed to the local atmosphere reads higher than the chart for the same temperature (roughly 0.5 psi per 1,000 ft); digital manifolds with an altitude setting correct for this. Values shown in inHg vac are vacuum (below atmospheric pressure). The chart describes saturated refrigerant only; it does not tell you whether a system is correctly charged by itself.

Worked example: superheat and subcooling with R-1233zd(E)

Superheat. Suction pressure at the service valve reads -6 psig. On this chart that pressure is a saturation temperature of about 40 °F. Your clamp thermometer on the suction line near the same point reads 52 °F, so superheat = 52 − 40 = 12 °F. Compare it with the target from the manufacturer’s charging chart or our target superheat calculator (fixed orifice systems).

Subcooling. Liquid line pressure reads 17 psig, which is a saturation temperature of about 105 °F. The liquid line measures 95 °F, so subcooling = 105 − 95 = 10 °F. TXV and EEV systems are normally charged to the subcooling printed on the outdoor unit’s rating plate. Do the math automatically with the superheat and subcooling calculators.

How this chart was made and checked

Pressures are computed with CoolProp 8, an open-source property library that implements the same Helmholtz equations of state used by NIST REFPROP, at 1 °F steps. We then checked the result against published charts: Hudson Technologies PT chart (12 points, max 0.41 °F). Across all 12 compared points the largest difference is 0.41 °F in saturation temperature (our pass limit is 1.0 °F, about the resolution of a good digital gauge). Points within 2 psi of atmospheric pressure are not compared because printed charts switch to vacuum units there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the R-1233zd(E) pressure at 40 °F?
R-1233zd(E) saturates at 12.6 inHg vac psig at 40 °F.
What is the R-1233zd(E) pressure at 100 °F?
The saturation pressure of R-1233zd(E) at 100 °F is 14.5 psig.
Is R-1233zd(E) flammable?
No. R-1233zd(E) is ASHRAE safety group A1 (no flame propagation). It can still displace oxygen and form toxic products in a flame.
