1" PVC Schedule 40 Conduit Fill

A 1-inch PVC Schedule 40 holds up to 25 × 12 AWG or 15 × 10 AWG THHN/THWN-2 conductors at the NEC 40% fill limit. Its internal area is 0.832 in² (40% = 0.333 in²).

Mixed sizes example

Three 10 AWG THHN plus one 12 AWG THHN ground: 3 × 0.0211 + 0.0133 = 0.0766 in², which is 9.2% of 1" PVC Schedule 40 (within the 40% limit). For mixed sizes always add the areas instead of using the same-size table.

Notes

  • Counts are for conductors only. Grounding conductors count toward fill.
  • With more than three current-carrying conductors, apply ampacity adjustment (NEC 310.15(C)(1)).
  • A nipple of 24 in. or less may be filled to 60%.

How many wires fit: Schedule 40 vs Schedule 80

At the 40% limit, 1 in. Schedule 40 PVC offers 0.333 sq in. of wire space and Schedule 80 offers 0.275 sq in., because the thicker wall of Schedule 80 leaves a smaller opening. Dividing by the THHN conductor areas in Chapter 9 Table 5 gives the maximum number of same-size wires:

Using PVC in practice

PVC is not a ground path, so count the equipment grounding conductor in the fill. Schedule 80 is the one to use where the conduit may be damaged, and expansion fittings are needed on long straight runs. The conduit comparison guide covers the rules, and the 40% rule guide explains the limit itself.

Schedule 40 vs Schedule 80 vs EMT at 1 in.

Thicker walls leave less room for wire. At the same trade size, the 40% area is 0.333 sq in. for Schedule 40 PVC, 0.275 sq in. for Schedule 80 PVC and 0.346 sq in. for EMT. With 12 AWG THHN (0.0133 sq in. each) that is 25 conductors in Schedule 40, 20 in Schedule 80 and 26 in EMT. Use the column for the wall thickness actually installed; underground and exposed runs are often Schedule 80, which holds fewer wires.

See the full comparison in EMT vs PVC vs rigid, and remember PVC expands and contracts with temperature, so long outdoor runs need expansion fittings under the NEC.
