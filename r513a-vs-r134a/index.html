R-513A vs R-134a

At 100 °F condensing, R-513A sits at 131.3 psig versus 124.2 psig for R-134a (+6%). At a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures are 39.8 and 35.0 psig. R-513A is safety group A1, R-134a is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressures are close enough that the same high-pressure gauges and hoses work, but you must switch to the matching PT scale or refrigerant setting on your digital manifold.

Glide. Both refrigerants have little or no glide.

Flammability. Both are A1 (no flame propagation), so no flammable-refrigerant procedures are needed, but standard refrigerant safety still applies.

Is R-513A a drop-in for R-134a? Treat no refrigerant as a pure drop-in. Some blends are approved retrofits, but oil, seals, metering devices, pressure controls and charge amount may change. Follow the refrigerant producer’s retrofit guidelines and the equipment manufacturer.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for R-513A and R-134a.
  • Charge blends as liquid and by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 7 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-513A run at higher pressure than R-134a?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-513A is about 7 psi higher (+6%) than R-134a; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by +4.8 psi.
Which is safer, R-513A or R-134a?
R-513A is ASHRAE group A1 and R-134a is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-513A and R-134a?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
