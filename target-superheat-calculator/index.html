Target Superheat Calculator

For fixed-orifice (piston) A/C systems: enter indoor wet bulb at the return and outdoor dry bulb at the condenser inlet to get the approximate target superheat. Then compare with your measured superheat.

Calculate target superheat

How the target is estimated

Fixed metering devices cannot adjust to load, so the correct superheat changes with the heat load on the evaporator (indoor wet bulb) and the condensing conditions (outdoor dry bulb). Manufacturers publish a charging table for each unit. A widely used approximation of those tables is:

Target superheat ≈ (3 × WBindoor − 80 − DBoutdoor) ÷ 2

Example: 63 °F indoor wet bulb and 85 °F outdoor: (189 − 80 − 85) ÷ 2 = 12 °F. If you measure 20 °F superheat the system is likely undercharged or short on indoor airflow-related load; if you measure 5 °F it may be overcharged or have low indoor airflow. Correct airflow first (typically about 400 CFM per ton unless the manufacturer specifies otherwise), then adjust charge in small steps and let the system settle.

Measuring the inputs correctly

  • Indoor wet bulb: measure in the return air close to the air handler with a psychrometer or a digital wet-bulb probe, after the system has run 15 minutes. Wet bulb includes humidity, which is the main load on the evaporator.
  • Outdoor dry bulb: measure the air entering the condenser coil in the shade, not the temperature reported by a weather app.
  • Measured superheat: suction pressure and suction line temperature at the outdoor service valve, converted with the superheat calculator.

Limits of the approximation

The formula reproduces the general shape of manufacturer charging tables for residential fixed-orifice systems with standard line sets. It does not account for unusual line lengths, airflow problems or units with special metering. When the manufacturer chart is on the unit or in the installation manual, use it; when the formula gives a number below about 5 °F, many manufacturers say the system should not be charged by superheat at those conditions.

Frequently asked questions

What is target superheat?
The superheat a fixed-orifice (piston or capillary) system should have at current indoor wet-bulb and outdoor dry-bulb temperatures when correctly charged.
What formula does this calculator use?
Target superheat ≈ (3 × indoor wet bulb − 80 − outdoor dry bulb) ÷ 2, a widely used approximation of manufacturers’ fixed-orifice charging tables. The equipment manufacturer’s chart always takes priority.
Does target superheat apply to TXV systems?
No. TXV and electronic expansion valve systems are charged by subcooling; the valve controls superheat.
Why does the result say below 5 °F?
At hot outdoor and dry indoor conditions the formula gives very low numbers. Many manufacturers say not to charge by superheat there; check airflow and use the manufacturer method.
