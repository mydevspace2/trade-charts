A2L Tools: Leak Detectors, Recovery Machines and What Changes

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

A2L refrigerants such as R-454B and R-32 are classed as mildly flammable. They need a strong ignition source and a high concentration to burn, but that is enough to change which tools you can put on the system. The good news: you do not need a whole new truck. Only the tools that touch the refrigerant circuit, or that could be an ignition source in a leak, need attention.

What “mildly flammable” means for your tools

ASHRAE Standard 34 puts refrigerants with low flammability and a low burning velocity in class 2L. Carrier’s A2L tools guide sums up the practical side: a direct flame and a high refrigerant concentration are needed to ignite it, a flame is unlikely to sustain itself, and its spread rate is slower than walking speed. The risk comes from tools with brushes, relays and switches that can spark inside a cloud of leaked refrigerant. That is why the rule of thumb from distributors and tool makers is: tools that touch the refrigerant circuit must be non-sparking and rated or listed for A2L use.

Recovery machines

A standard recovery machine with a brushed motor or an open pressure switch can spark. A2L-rated machines use brushless motors and sealed electrical components. Look for a listing that names A2L or flammable refrigerants; UL 1963, the safety standard for recovery and recycling equipment, covers equipment for flammable refrigerants when it is investigated to its Supplement SB. EPA also requires recovery equipment used on stationary systems to be certified (normally to AHRI 740). Many current machines handle both A1 and A2L refrigerants and have a purge or self-clear mode so you do not carry R-410A into an R-454B system or the other way round.

Leak detectors

Use the electronic detector to find the area of the leak, then confirm the exact spot with bubble solution, which has no spark risk at all. Electronic “sniffers” for A2L work should be fully sealed. Check that the detector is specified for HFO blends; some older heated-diode sensors were designed around HFCs and may read R-454B or R-32 poorly. Several manufacturers list compliance with EN 14624 or SAE J2791/J2913 sensitivity tests on A2L-capable models.

Vacuum pumps and micron gauges

Evacuation itself does not change: pull the system down and prove it with a micron gauge, because a compound gauge cannot read at micron levels. The pump must be non-sparking, since at the start of evacuation it may move residual refrigerant through its motor area. JB Industries recommends a triple evacuation on A2L systems.

Cylinders, adapters and hoses

  • Red band: A2L refrigerant and recovery cylinders carry a red band or red top and warning labels.
  • Relief valve: they use a pressure relief valve instead of a rupture disc.
  • Left-hand threads: the valve outlet is reverse-threaded, so you need a reverse-thread adapter for your hoses.
  • One refrigerant per cylinder: an R-454B or R-32 recovery cylinder is for that refrigerant only; R-410A still needs its own.

Tools that usually do not change

Scales, temperature clamps, psychrometers, wireless probes that never contact refrigerant, tubing cutters, flaring and swaging tools all carry over. Carrier’s list adds one item many techs had not carried before: a multipurpose fire extinguisher on the truck. Press-fit and mechanical fittings are an option where brazing is impractical, but brazing on an evacuated, recovered system purged with nitrogen is still normal practice.

A checklist before your first A2L call

  1. Recovery machine listed for A2L, and a red-band recovery cylinder for each A2L refrigerant you service.
  2. Reverse-thread adapters for cylinders.
  3. Sealed, A2L-capable electronic leak detector plus bubble solution.
  4. Non-sparking vacuum pump and a micron gauge.
  5. Digital manifold updated with R-454B and R-32, or analog gauges with those scales.
  6. A fire extinguisher, ventilation plan and the manufacturer’s A2L installation and service instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a new recovery machine for R-454B and R-32?
You need one that is non-sparking and listed for A2L refrigerants. Many current machines handle both A1 and A2L refrigerants with a purge mode between them.
Can I use my old leak detector on A2L refrigerants?
Only if it is sealed against sparking and rated to detect HFO and A2L blends. Bubble solution is always safe for pinpointing a leak.
Why do A2L cylinders have left-hand threads?
The reverse threads, red band and relief valve make A2L cylinders hard to confuse with A1 refrigerant cylinders. A reverse-thread adapter connects your hoses.
Do I need a new vacuum pump?
You need a non-sparking pump, typically with a brushless DC motor. Many newer pumps already qualify; check the manufacturer’s A2L listing.
Which tools do not need to change?
Tools that never touch the refrigerant or create sparks, such as scales, clamps, psychrometers, cutters and flaring tools, usually carry over.
