Propane vs Natural Gas Pipe Sizing: Same House, Different Pipe

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Propane carries more energy per cubic foot than natural gas, so the same appliance load needs a smaller pipe on propane. The tables are also written in different units, which trips up many people.

Different units, different gas

The IFGC natural gas tables list capacity in cubic feet of gas per hour (CFH) at a specific gravity of 0.60. The propane tables list thousands of Btu per hour (MBH) at a specific gravity of 1.50. To size natural gas you convert the appliance input to CFH by dividing by the heating value, commonly assumed to be 1,000 Btu per cubic foot. For propane you can use the appliance input in MBH directly.

Worked example: 100,000 Btu/h at 30 ft

Take a 100,000 Btu/h total load, longest run 30 ft, at low pressure with a 0.5 in. w.c. drop.

GasLoad in table unitsPipeCapacity at 30 ft
Natural gas (CFH)100,000 ÷ 1,000 = 100 CFH3/4 in.199 CFH (1/2 in. holds only 95)
Propane (MBH)100 MBH1/2 in.160 MBH

The natural gas run needs 3/4 in. black iron because the 1/2 in. pipe carries just 95 CFH at 30 ft. The propane run gets away with 1/2 in. because the same table row allows 160 MBH. Convert the natural gas capacity to Btu at 1,000 per cubic foot and 3/4 in. is 199 MBH, so propane at 11 in. w.c. through 1/2 in. gets nearly the same Btu flow as natural gas through the next size up.

Pressure matters more

A 2 psi system with a 1 psi drop carries far more through the same pipe. At 30 ft, 1/2 in. black iron carries 869 CFH of natural gas at 2 psi, against 95 CFH at low pressure. For propane, 1/2 in. carries 1480 MBH at 2 psi, against 160 MBH at 11 in. w.c. That is why long runs to a detached building or a large load often use 2 psi with a regulator at each appliance.

Step by step

  1. Add up the Btu/h input of every appliance on the line.
  2. Natural gas: divide by the heating value to get CFH. Propane: keep MBH.
  3. Measure the longest length from the meter or tank regulator to the farthest appliance.
  4. Choose the table row at or just above that length and use it for every section.
  5. Pick the first pipe size whose capacity is at or above the load.

Cautions

  • Use the table for the right pressure and drop. Low pressure propane is usually 11 in. w.c. at the appliance side.
  • Fittings add equivalent length; allow for them on long or twisty runs.
  • Do not use natural gas orifices on propane appliances; appliances must be converted with the manufacturer’s kit.
  • Local code and the gas supplier have the last word.

Frequently asked questions

Why is propane pipe smaller than natural gas pipe?
Propane holds more Btu per cubic foot and is delivered at higher pressure, so the same pipe carries more energy.
What units do the propane tables use?
Thousands of Btu per hour (MBH), so you use the appliance input directly.
How do I convert Btu/h to CFH for natural gas?
Divide by the gas heating value, commonly assumed to be 1,000 Btu per cubic foot.
Can I use natural gas appliances on propane?
Only with a manufacturer conversion kit; orifices and pressure differ.
When should I use a 2 psi system?
On long runs or large loads where low-pressure pipe would be oversized, with a regulator at each appliance.
