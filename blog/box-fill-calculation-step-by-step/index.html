Box Fill Calculation Step by Step (NEC 314.16 with Real Boxes)

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

A box that is too full means crowded wires, damaged insulation and a failed inspection. NEC 314.16 sets the rule: add up the volume allowance for every conductor, clamp, device and ground, and compare it with the box’s marked volume in cubic inches. This guide shows the counting rules, then works two real examples against box volumes from Table 314.16(A).

Step 1: Know the volume per conductor

Table 314.16(B) gives each conductor size a volume allowance:

16 AWG1.75
14 AWG2.00
12 AWG2.25
10 AWG2.50
8 AWG3.00
6 AWG5.00

Clamps that sit outside the box and plastic-box clamps already included in the marked volume add nothing. Small fittings such as locknuts and bushings need no allowance.

Example 1: A receptacle fed through

A single-gang box holds one duplex receptacle and two 12/2 NM cables with ground, with internal clamps.

ItemCountVolume
12 AWG conductors (2 hots + 2 neutrals)4 × 2.259.00
Internal clamps1 × 2.252.25
Receptacle yoke2 × 2.254.50
Grounds (2 count as one)1 × 2.252.25
Total8 allowances18.00 cu in.

Our box table lists a 3 × 2 × 3-1/2 device box at 18.0 cu in., which fits exactly, and a 4 × 1-1/2 square box at 21.0 cu in., which fits with room to spare. A shallow 3 × 2 × 2-1/2 box (12.5 cu in.) does not.

Example 2: Two switches on three 14/2 cables

A two-gang opening holds two switches with three 14/2 cables with ground.

ItemCountVolume
14 AWG conductors6 × 2.0012.00
Internal clamps1 × 2.002.00
Two switch yokes2 × 2 × 2.008.00
Grounds (3 count as one)1 × 2.002.00
Total12 allowances24.00 cu in.

A 4-11/16 × 1-1/4 square box holds 25.5 cu in. and passes; a 4 × 1-1/2 square (21.0) does not. A 4 × 2-1/8 square at 30.3 cu in. also passes with room for a plaster ring extension.

Mixed wire sizes

When 12 AWG and 14 AWG share a box, count each conductor at its own size; clamps and grounds use the largest conductor in the box, and each device yoke uses the largest conductor connected to that device. A switch on a 14 AWG circuit counts at 2.00 even if a 12 AWG circuit is elsewhere in the box.

Common mistakes

  • Counting every ground separately; up to four count as one, and in recent editions each extra adds a quarter allowance.
  • Counting the device as one instead of two allowances.
  • Counting pigtails that start and end inside the box.
  • Forgetting the volume marked on the box already includes extensions you did not install.
  • Using a box with a barrier without calculating each space separately.

Frequently asked questions

How many cubic inches per 12 AWG wire?
2.25 cubic inches per conductor, from Table 314.16(B).
How does a device count in box fill?
Each yoke counts as two conductor allowances, sized by the largest conductor connected to it.
Do ground wires count in box fill?
All equipment grounding conductors together count as one allowance of the largest ground, with extra allowance when more than four enter.
Do wire nuts or pigtails count?
Wire connectors need no allowance and pigtails that stay inside the box count zero.
Do cable clamps count?
Internal clamps count once total at the largest conductor; external clamps count nothing.
