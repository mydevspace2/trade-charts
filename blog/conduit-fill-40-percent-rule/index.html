Conduit Fill Explained: The NEC 40% Rule With Examples

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Conduit fill is a simple area comparison: the conductors’ total cross-sectional area must not exceed a percentage of the raceway’s internal area. The percentages live in NEC Chapter 9, Table 1.

The three limits

Number of conductorsMaximum fill
153%
231%
3 or more40%

Two conductors get the lowest limit because two round wires side by side can jam in a bend. The internal areas of each raceway type and trade size are in Chapter 9 Table 4; conductor areas (with insulation) are in Table 5.

Worked example: same size

3/4 in EMT has an internal area of 0.533 in²; 40% is 0.213 in². One 12 AWG THHN is 0.0133 in². 0.213 ÷ 0.0133 = 16.0, so 16 conductors fit. Chapter 9 Note 7 lets you round up only when the remainder is 0.8 or more, and only when all conductors are the same size.

Worked example: mixed sizes

Three 8 AWG THHN (0.0366 in² each) plus one 10 AWG THHN ground (0.0211 in²) = 0.1309 in². In 3/4 in EMT that is 24.6% — fine. In 1/2 in EMT (0.304 in², 40% = 0.122 in²) it is 43% — too full. Use our conduit fill calculator to add any mix and see the smallest raceway that works.

Details that catch people

  • Grounds count. Equipment grounding conductors, insulated or bare, are included (Note 3).
  • Nipples. A raceway section 24 in or shorter between enclosures may be filled to 60% (Note 4).
  • Cables. A multiconductor cable counts as one conductor using its outside diameter (Note 9).
  • Raceway type matters. 1 in PVC Schedule 80 holds noticeably less than 1 in EMT; check the right chart.
  • Fill is not ampacity. More than three current-carrying conductors require the 310.15(C)(1) adjustment, which may force larger wire and therefore larger conduit.

Quick reference

For a fast answer without arithmetic, open the same-size chart for your raceway, for example the 3/4 in EMT page (16 × 12 AWG THHN) or the 1 in PVC page. For mixed sizes use the calculator.
