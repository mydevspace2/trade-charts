Printable Truck Reference: Which Charts to Carry in Your Truck

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Every trade has a handful of lookups that come up again and again, and the page you need is rarely the one you have open. Here is a short carry list, grouped by trade, with the page on this site for each. Print the pages you use most or save them to your phone’s home screen.

HVAC and refrigeration

  • Pressure-temperature (PT) charts: the master list of refrigerants. The big three are R-410A, R-22 and R-134A, and newer systems use R-454B.
  • Target superheat calculator for fixed-orifice systems.
  • HVAC calculators for the everyday maths.
  • HVAC age by serial number when a customer asks how old the unit is.

Electrical

  • Conduit fill calculator for how many conductors fit a raceway.
  • Box fill calculator for device boxes.
  • Voltage drop calculator for long runs.
  • Conduit offset multipliers for bending.
  • The electrical hub for wire ampacity pages by AWG size.

Plumbing and gas

  • Gas pipe sizing tables from the IFGC, including propane iron pipe, CSST and polyethylene.
  • Water heater age by serial number for warranty and replacement talks.

Welding

  • MIG settings calculator and the guide to MIG settings without a door chart.
  • TIG amperage chart and tungsten sizes and amperage.

How to print or save

  1. Open the page and use your browser’s Print command; choose “Save as PDF” to keep a copy on your phone.
  2. On a phone, use Share, then “Add to Home Screen” for one-tap access.
  3. Keep one laminated page in the truck for the chart you reach for most, and check the source line on the page before relying on a number for a permit job.

A sample truck binder by trade

If you only want a one-page-per-topic binder, start with these and add as jobs demand. A residential HVAC tech needs the R-410A and R-454B charts plus one for R-22, because older systems are still in service. An electrician doing remodel work lives in conduit fill, box fill and voltage drop. A plumber or gas fitter wants the pipe sizing table that matches the fuel and the pipe material, and the water heater age page for warranty questions. A welder wants the MIG and TIG starting-point pages when the machine chart is missing.

Check before you rely on a number

  • Match the refrigerant on the nameplate to the chart; mixing them up is the commonest mistake.
  • For gas sizing, confirm the pressure, length and pipe type against the table heading.
  • For conductors, confirm your local code edition and the termination temperature rating.
  • For welding settings, treat them as starting points and test on scrap.

Why a reference, not a rulebook

These pages collect published tables from manufacturers, EPA and code documents so you do not have to hunt through PDFs. Your local code, the equipment nameplate and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first, and every page names its source so you can check it.

Sources

Each page linked above lists its own published sources at the bottom, such as the International Fuel Gas Code tables, NEC tables, EPA refrigerant data and Miller Electric guidance.
