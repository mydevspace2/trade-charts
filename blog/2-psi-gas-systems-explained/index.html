2 PSI Gas Systems Explained: When and How to Size One

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Most houses run gas at low pressure, about half a pound or less, which is roughly 7 in. of water column at the appliance. A 2 psi system raises the pressure in the piping and puts a regulator at each appliance, so a much smaller pipe can carry the same load. It is common for long runs, big loads and homes using CSST.

Low pressure vs 2 psi

Gas moves because pressure falls along the pipe. The IFGC tables size pipe so the pressure drop stays within an allowed amount. A low pressure system can only spare a fraction of an inch of water column, so the pipe must be large. A 2 psi system can spare a full 1 psi, which is about 28 in. w.c., so the same job fits in far smaller pipe.

Those numbers come from our natural gas chart and 2 psi chart, which copy IFGC Tables 402.4(2) and 402.4(5). At 30 ft, half-inch pipe carries 869 CFH on a 2 psi system but only 95 CFH at low pressure.

The catch: regulators

Appliances are built for low pressure. Each one needs a line regulator that steps the 2 psi down to the appliance pressure, normally 7 in. w.c. for natural gas. The code requires the regulator to be listed for the job, sized for the load, and installed where it can be reached and vented as the manufacturer instructs. Check the adopted code in your area for exact placement and vent rules. Many jurisdictions also want a pressure test at a higher pressure than a low pressure system.

Where 2 psi makes sense

  • Long runs from the meter to a detached building or far corner of a large house.
  • High total loads such as a multi-appliance home with a pool heater or generator.
  • Systems using CSST, where manufacturers publish 2 psi tables.
  • Retrofits where pulling larger low pressure pipe would be costly.

For a small house with short runs, low pressure pipe is usually simpler and cheaper because it needs no extra regulators.

Worked example

A furnace and water heater total 100,000 Btu/h. For natural gas that is 100 CFH. The longest run is 30 ft.

  1. Low pressure: the first pipe at or above 100 CFH in the 30 ft row is 3/4 in. (199 CFH), since 1/2 in. only gives 95 CFH.
  2. 2 psi: 1/2 in. already carries 869 CFH, far above 100 CFH, so half-inch pipe is enough.

Run your own numbers with the gas pipe sizing calculator. The same logic applies to propane: compare the 2 psi propane chart, and see propane vs natural gas sizing.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using a 2 psi table on a system with no regulator at the appliance.
  • Mixing tables: each pressure and drop has its own table.
  • Forgetting that 2 psi tables use a 1 psi drop, not 0.5 in. w.c.
  • Skipping the manufacturer instructions for CSST at elevated pressure.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 2 psi gas system?
A piping system operated at about 2 psi with a regulator at each appliance, allowing smaller pipe than low pressure.
Do I need a regulator on a 2 psi system?
Yes. Appliances need a regulator that steps pressure down, normally to 7 in. w.c. for natural gas.
What pressure drop do 2 psi tables use?
1 psi, versus 0.5 in. w.c. in the common low pressure table.
Is 2 psi cheaper to install?
Pipe is smaller, but regulators add cost, so it pays off mainly on long runs and large loads.
Can I convert an existing system to 2 psi?
Only with the right regulators, a code-compliant pressure test and approval from your inspector and gas supplier.
