Converting Btu/h to CFH for Gas Pipe Sizing (Step by Step)

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Appliance labels list gas input in Btu per hour, but natural gas pipe sizing tables are in cubic feet per hour (CFH). One simple division connects them. Getting this step wrong is the most common reason a gas line ends up the wrong size.

The formula

CFH = appliance input in Btu/h ÷ heating value of the gas in Btu per cubic foot. The IFGC sizing procedure uses a typical natural gas heating value of 1,000 Btu per cubic foot unless the gas supplier gives a different number. So a 40,000 Btu/h water heater needs 40 CFH.

Totaling several appliances

ApplianceInput (Btu/h)CFH
Furnace80,00080
Water heater40,00040
Range65,00065
Dryer22,00022
Total207,000207

Add the Btu/h first, then divide once. With a longest run of 40 ft, the Schedule 40 low pressure table needs a pipe carrying at least 207 CFH in the 40 ft row; check it on the natural gas chart or enter the load in the calculator.

Propane is different

The IFGC propane tables are written in thousands of Btu per hour (MBH). You use the Btu/h input directly, so no heating-value division is needed: 207,000 Btu/h is 207 MBH. Do not feed a propane load into a natural gas table in CFH. See propane vs natural gas sizing.

Step by step

  1. Read the input rating (not output or efficiency) from each appliance nameplate.
  2. Add the Btu/h of all appliances on that pipe section.
  3. Divide by 1,000 for natural gas (or by the supplier’s heating value).
  4. Look up the longest length and choose a pipe that carries at least that many CFH.

Common mistakes

  • Using output Btu/h instead of input.
  • Dividing each appliance and rounding down before adding.
  • Reading MBH tables as CFH.
  • Forgetting future appliances; a little oversizing is cheaper than redoing the line.

For CSST the same CFH number goes into the EHD table.

Quick reference examples

  • 30,000 Btu/h gas fireplace: 30 CFH.
  • 50,000 Btu/h tank water heater: 50 CFH.
  • 150,000 Btu/h tankless water heater: 150 CFH, often the biggest load in a house, so size for it first.
  • 35,000 Btu/h outdoor grill: 35 CFH.

Why total load matters

The IFGC longest length method sizes every section for the load it carries, using the longest run from the meter. A trunk line feeding all appliances carries the total, while a branch to one dryer carries only the dryer. Convert each appliance first, then add them section by section. A common shortcut is to add every appliance, find the longest length, and size the whole system from one row. That is conservative and often fine for houses, but it can oversize branches.

A note on units

You may see MBH on labels, meaning thousands of Btu per hour, so 80 MBH equals 80,000 Btu/h and 80 CFH of natural gas. Others write kBtu/h. Never confuse MBH with MMBtu, which is a million Btu.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Btu/h to CFH?
Divide by the gas heating value. For natural gas the IFGC procedure assumes 1,000 Btu per cubic foot.
How many CFH is a 100,000 Btu/h furnace?
About 100 CFH on natural gas at 1,000 Btu per cubic foot.
Do propane tables use CFH?
The IFGC propane tables use thousands of Btu per hour (MBH).
Should I use input or output Btu?
Use input rating from the nameplate.
What if my gas has a different heating value?
Use the value from your gas supplier in the same division.
