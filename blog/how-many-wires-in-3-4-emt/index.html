How Many Wires Fit in 3/4 EMT? Worked Examples by Wire Size

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

The quick answer for ordinary THHN wire in 3/4 inch EMT: 21 conductors of 14 AWG, 16 of 12 AWG, 10 of 10 AWG, 5 of 8 AWG or 4 of 6 AWG, all at the NEC 40% fill limit. Those numbers come straight from the Chapter 9 tables, but the real answer on a job also depends on what else is in the pipe, how many wires are carrying current and how the ampacity is derated. This page shows the math and works three examples.

The inputs

NEC Chapter 9 Table 4 gives 3/4 trade size EMT an internal area of 0.533 sq in. (inside diameter 0.824 in.). For more than two conductors the maximum fill is 40% of that area, which is 0.213 sq in. Table 5 gives the area of each conductor; for THHN the values are in the table below. The count is the usable area divided by the wire area, always rounded down, because you cannot install a fraction of a wire.

Example 1: a home-run of 12 AWG circuits

Suppose you want to pull several 20-amp, 120-volt circuits in one pipe. Each circuit needs a hot, a neutral and (usually) a ground, and all three count toward fill. Eight conductors of 12 AWG take 0.106 sq in., well inside the 0.213 limit. Even 16 conductors of 12 AWG (0.213 sq in.) fit. So fill is not the constraint here; ampacity derating is, as explained below.

Example 2: mixed 12 and 10 AWG

You need four 12 AWG and three 10 AWG THHN conductors. Add the areas: 4 × 0.0133 + 3 × 0.0211 = 0.1165 sq in. Compare it with 0.213: it fits, with 0.097 sq in. to spare. For a mix you always add areas rather than counting wires.

Example 3: how many 6 AWG feeders?

Four 6 AWG THHN conductors use 0.2028 sq in. A fifth would be 0.2535 sq in., over the 0.213 limit. If those four are the neutral plus three hots of a subpanel feeder, the ground goes in too, and Table 5 area must include it; a smaller bare or insulated ground adds to the total, so check it with the calculator before you buy the wire.

Grounding conductors and nipples

Equipment grounding conductors count in the fill calculation when they are installed in the raceway. NEC Chapter 9 Table 1 Note 4 also allows 60% fill for a raceway section 24 inches or shorter (a nipple), and our 40% rule guide covers that note and the rounding rule in detail.

Fill is not the same as ampacity

Electrical Contractor magazine points out that the ampacity values in Table 310.15(B)(16) (now 310.16) assume no more than three current-carrying conductors in the raceway and a 30 °C (86 °F) ambient. Put 16 current-carrying wires in a 3/4 pipe and the code requires you to adjust the ampacity down, often by half or more. In practice that means that you may physically fit 16 wires but need a larger wire size, or two pipes, to carry the load. Our wire ampacity chart gives base values, and the voltage drop calculator checks long runs.

Pulling tips for a full 3/4 pipe

  • Pull all conductors together rather than adding wires later; a second pull drags on the first set.
  • Keep bends under the NEC limit of 360 degrees total between pull points, offsets included.
  • Use listed wire-pulling lubricant for the heavier counts and larger sizes.
  • If the count is close to the limit, step up to 1 inch EMT. It holds roughly 62% more wire area than 3/4 inch (0.346 vs 0.213 sq in.).

Rules and tables vary slightly by code edition and by local amendment, so confirm with your inspector before ordering material.

Frequently asked questions

How many 12 AWG THHN wires fit in 3/4 EMT?
16, based on 0.213 sq in. of usable area (40% of 0.533) divided by 0.0133 sq in. per wire.
How many 10 AWG wires fit in 3/4 EMT?
10 conductors of THHN fit at the 40% limit.
Do ground wires count toward conduit fill?
Yes. Equipment grounding conductors installed in the raceway are included in the fill calculation.
Is the number of wires limited by ampacity too?
Yes. Table ampacities assume no more than three current-carrying conductors; more than that requires an ampacity adjustment.
Can I fill a short nipple to 60%?
Yes. A raceway section 24 inches or shorter may be filled to 60% under Chapter 9 Table 1 Note 4.
