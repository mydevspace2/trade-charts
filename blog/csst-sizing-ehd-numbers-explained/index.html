CSST Sizing and EHD Numbers Explained (With a Worked Example)

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

CSST, corrugated stainless steel tubing, is not sized by inches. It is sized by an EHD number such as 13, 18 or 25. A bigger EHD means more capacity. Here is what that number means and how to use it with the code tables.

What EHD means

The IFGC defines EHD as Equivalent Hydraulic Diameter, a measure of the relative hydraulic efficiency between different tubing sizes. The greater the EHD, the greater the gas capacity. Because corrugated tubing flows differently from smooth steel pipe, the EHD gives it a rating you can compare across manufacturers. It is not a physical diameter, so do not read it like a nominal pipe size.

10 ft32 CFH44 CFH82 CFH95 CFH161 CFH192 CFH
30 ft18 CFH25 CFH47 CFH55 CFH96 CFH112 CFH
50 ft13 CFH19 CFH37 CFH42 CFH75 CFH87 CFH

These are IFGC Table 402.4(15) values for natural gas under 2 psi with a 0.5 in. w.c. drop; the full table is on our CSST sizing chart. Notice the drop with length: EHD 18 carries 82 CFH at 10 ft but only 47 CFH at 30 ft.

How to size CSST step by step

  1. Add up the CFH of every appliance on the run. For natural gas, divide each Btu/h by 1,000 (see our Btu to CFH guide).
  2. Measure the length from the meter or regulator to the farthest outlet.
  3. Find that length, or the next longer row, in the table.
  4. Move across the row to the first EHD with capacity at or above the load.

Worked example

A furnace and water heater total 60 CFH at 30 ft. The 30 ft row gives EHD 13 = 18, EHD 15 = 25, EHD 18 = 47 and EHD 19 = 55. The first that covers 60 CFH is EHD 19 (55 CFH).

Fittings and bends

The table already includes losses for four 90-degree bends and two end fittings. For each extra bend or fitting, add 1.3 ft of tubing length to your measurement (L = 1.3n) and re-read the table at the longer length.

Manufacturer rules still apply

Code tables give a general sizing basis, but each CSST brand is listed with its own installation instructions covering bonding, strike protection, support and fittings. Follow the manufacturer manual and local code, and use a certified installer. For higher pressure see the 2 psi CSST chart and 2 psi systems explained; for propane use the propane CSST chart.

Why capacity drops so quickly with length

Gas flowing through a long run loses pressure to friction. Because the table holds the pressure drop fixed at 0.5 in. w.c., every extra foot reduces how much gas the tubing can pass. Doubling the run does not halve the capacity exactly, but the table shows the trend: EHD 18 carries 82 CFH at 10 ft, 47 CFH at 30 ft and 37 CFH at 50 ft. Keep trunk lines short and use home-run or branch layouts when you can.

Comparing CSST to black iron

A rough comparison helps when planning a retrofit. At 30 ft, 1/2 in. Schedule 40 pipe carries 95 CFH at low pressure, while EHD 18 CSST carries 47 CFH and EHD 25 carries 112 CFH. Manufacturers map their nominal sizes to EHD differently, so always check the brand’s own chart. You can compare pipe tables on the gas pipe sizing page.

Frequently asked questions

What does EHD mean in CSST?
Equivalent Hydraulic Diameter, a relative measure of the tubing’s hydraulic efficiency. Higher EHD means more capacity.
Is EHD the same as pipe diameter?
No. It is a flow rating, not a physical size.
How many feet do extra fittings add?
The IFGC note adds 1.3 ft of tubing length per additional bend or fitting beyond those built into the table.
Which table do I use for CSST?
IFGC Tables 402.4(15) to 402.4(17), depending on pressure and drop.
Can I use code tables instead of the manufacturer manual?
Follow the manufacturer’s listed instructions and local code; the tables are a sizing basis only.
