Sizing Wire for a Detached Garage Subpanel (60 A at 100 ft Example)

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Running power to a detached garage or workshop is one of the most common residential projects, and the wire size is rarely just about ampacity. Over 100 feet, voltage drop often decides the size, and the code adds requirements for a separate grounding electrode and an isolated neutral at the garage. This guide walks through a 60 A, 240 V feeder over 100 ft with copper conductors, using our table data.

Step 1: Decide the feeder rating

Add up the garage loads (lights, receptacles, a compressor, a welder, an EV charger) using a load calculation and pick a feeder breaker size. We use 60 A for the example. The feeder is a 2-pole breaker in the house panel that feeds a subpanel in the garage with its own main or disconnect. A disconnecting means is required at or in the separate building.

Step 2: Check ampacity first

For a 60 A breaker that has no marked terminal rating, 110.14(C) limits you to the 60 °C column for circuits of 100 A or less unless the equipment is marked for 75 °C. Most breakers and lugs today are marked 60/75 °C, so the 75 °C column usually applies. From our ampacity chart, copper at 75 °C: 8 AWG = 50 A, 6 AWG = 65 A, 4 AWG = 85 A. For a 60 A load, 6 AWG copper (65 A) is the minimum, assuming no derating for bundling or temperature, which you check with the derating guide.

Step 3: Check voltage drop over 100 ft

The NEC recommends in informational notes that feeder and branch-circuit voltage drop each stay within 3%, and no more than 5% total (our 3% rule guide covers this). For a single-phase 2-wire feeder the drop is 2 × resistance per foot × length × current, using Chapter 9 Table 8 resistances (uncoated copper). At 60 A and 100 ft one way on 240 V:

8 AWG0.7789.33.9%
6 AWG0.4915.92.5%
4 AWG0.3083.71.5%
3 AWG0.2452.91.2%

With 6 AWG the drop is about 2.5%, under the 3% guideline, so it works for a full 60 A load. If the garage is 200 ft away the drop doubles to about 4.9% and you would step up to 4 AWG. Check your own length with the voltage drop calculator. Real loads are rarely a continuous 60 A, so these are worst-case figures.

Step 4: Four wires, isolated neutral

For new work, the feeder to a detached garage has four conductors: two ungrounded (hots), a grounded neutral and a separate equipment grounding conductor [250.32(B)(1)]. At the garage panel, the neutral bar stays isolated from the enclosure and the ground bar; the neutral-to-ground bond exists only at the service in the main building. The older 3-wire feeder with a bonded neutral at the outbuilding was removed from the code for new installations in 2008. If you bonded the neutral at the garage, neutral current would flow on the ground wire and any metal conduit or piping between the buildings.

The equipment grounding conductor is sized from Table 250.122 based on the feeder breaker. For a 60 A breaker that is 10 AWG copper.

Step 5: Ground rods at the garage

250.32(A) requires a grounding electrode system at a separate building supplied by a feeder or by more than one branch circuit. A concrete-encased electrode, if present, counts; otherwise two ground rods are the common solution, and the grounding electrode conductor lands on the ground bar. The only exception is a building served by a single branch circuit that includes an equipment grounding conductor.

Step 6: Wiring method, burial and GFCI

Conductors can run in PVC conduit underground or in a cable listed for direct burial; see Table 300.5 for burial depth, which depends on the wiring method and whether the run is under a driveway. THWN-2 in PVC is the usual choice, and our conduit comparison covers the pros and cons. Receptacles in a garage need GFCI protection under 210.8, and the garage needs at least one 20 A circuit for receptacles [210.11(C)(4)].

Quick checklist

  1. Pick the feeder size from a load calculation.
  2. Choose wire by ampacity (75 °C column), then by voltage drop at your length.
  3. Four conductors: two hots, neutral, EGC from Table 250.122.
  4. Isolate neutral and ground at the garage panel; add a disconnect.
  5. Install ground rods or a concrete-encased electrode.
  6. Get a permit and inspection; local rules and the adopted NEC edition decide.

Frequently asked questions

What size wire for a 60 amp subpanel?
By ampacity, 6 AWG copper (65 A at 75°C). Check voltage drop for long runs; at 100 ft and 60 A, 6 AWG gives about 2.5% drop.
Does a detached garage need a ground rod?
Generally yes: a feeder or more than one branch circuit requires a grounding electrode at the building under 250.32(A).
Should the neutral and ground be bonded in a garage subpanel?
No. With a 4-wire feeder, the neutral bar is isolated and bonding happens only at the main service.
Can I run a 3-wire feeder to a garage?
Not for new work. The allowance was removed in the 2008 NEC; use two hots, a neutral and a ground.
Is a disconnect required at the garage?
Yes, a disconnecting means is required at or in the separate building.
