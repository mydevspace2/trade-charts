How Old Is My AC Unit? Serial Number Basics for 5 Major Brands

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

The manufacture date of an air conditioner, heat pump or furnace is printed on the unit, but not as a date. It is hidden inside the serial number. Each brand uses its own layout, so the first job is knowing which characters to read. Here is how to find the data plate, decode five major brands, and what the answer means for repair-or-replace decisions.

Step 1: find the data plate

For a central air conditioner or heat pump, the nameplate is usually on the outdoor condenser, on a side or back panel. EPA’s homeowner guidance says the same plate normally lists the refrigerant too. Furnaces and air handlers carry theirs inside the blower door. Copy the serial number, not the model number printed beside it. They look alike and people mix them up all the time.

Step 2: match the brand to its date code

The Goodman rule comes from Goodman’s retailer documentation (2110 means October 2021). The Lennox layout, including the 59 21 CO 05716 example, is from Lennox’s model and serial identification guide. Carrier and Bryant’s week-then-year pattern appears in an Energy Trust of Oregon equipment age guide (3298xxxxxx is the 32nd week of 1998). Several brands, including Carrier and Trane, do not publish a residential serial format, which is why this table says so where it applies. For a warranty claim or insurance question, ask the manufacturer with the full serial number.

Worked examples

Each example is read as of November 2026.

  • Carrier 3112U12345: digits 1–2 are the week (31), digits 3–4 the year (12), so week 31 of 2012, around early August. The unit is about 14 years old.
  • Goodman 1306123456: year 13, month 06, so June 2013, about 13 years old.
  • Lennox 5921CO05716: plant 59 is Marshalltown, Iowa, year 21 is 2021, and month letter C is March. About 5 years old. Note that the first two digits are a plant, not a date.

What the age tells you

Refrigerant. EPA says equipment containing R-22 could not be manufactured in the U.S. after January 1, 2010. A central air conditioner built before 2010 very likely holds R-22; one built from 2010 onward most likely holds R-410A, which EPA calls the most common alternative in new residential systems. New systems from 2025 increasingly use lower-GWP refrigerants such as R-454B. Always confirm on the nameplate. If you find R-22, read our guide to repairing, retrofitting or replacing an R-22 system, and see the R-22 PT chart, R-410A PT chart and R-454B PT chart for the pressures.

Lifespan. The U.S. Department of Energy puts a central air conditioner at 15 to 20 years. ENERGY STAR suggests having a contractor evaluate a heat pump or air conditioner once it is more than 10 years old, and a furnace or boiler once it is more than 15. Age is a reason to ask questions, not an automatic verdict: frequent repairs and rising bills matter as much as the calendar.

Warranty. Manufacturers generally start the warranty from the installation date when the unit was registered, and from the manufacture date when it was not. A unit can also sit in a warehouse for months, so the stamped date is when it was built, not when it was installed.

Bonus: read the capacity from the model number

Energy Trust’s guide gives a rule of thumb for size. Manufacturers usually put the nominal capacity in Btu/h (in thousands) somewhere in the model number as a two-digit number divisible by 6. Divide it by 12 to get tons. For example, Energy Trust’s sample model 56BAB0042000A0 contains 42, which means 42,000 Btu/h, or 3.5 tons. Some brands use other codes, so treat it as a quick estimate. To check whether the ductwork and charge match that size, use the HVAC calculators.

Common mistakes

  • Reading the model number instead of the serial number.
  • Applying one brand’s format to another brand’s unit, such as reading a Lennox plant code as a year.
  • Forgetting the decade. A two-digit year such as 05 can only be 2005 on a modern plate, but older serials from the 1970s and 1980s use letter codes, so a very old unit needs the brand-specific chart.
  • Treating the manufacture date as the install date.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the serial number on an AC unit?
On the data plate of the outdoor condenser, usually on a side or back panel. Furnaces and air handlers carry it inside the blower door.
How do I read a Goodman serial number?
The first four digits are the year then the month. For example 2110 means October 2021.
How do I read a Carrier serial number?
Carrier and Bryant units commonly start with the week then the year, so 3298 is week 32 of 1998. Carrier does not publish the format, so confirm for warranty claims.
How long does a central air conditioner last?
The U.S. Department of Energy puts it at 15 to 20 years. ENERGY STAR suggests having a contractor evaluate one past 10 years.
Is the manufacture date the install date?
No. Units can wait in a warehouse for months. Warranties usually count from the registered install date.
