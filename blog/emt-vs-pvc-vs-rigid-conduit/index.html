EMT vs PVC vs Rigid Conduit: Which Raceway to Choose

Guide · Updated November 13, 2026

Choosing between EMT, PVC and rigid metal conduit (RMC) is mostly a question of where the pipe will live. Indoors and protected, EMT is the cheap, fast answer. Underground or in a wet, corrosive spot, PVC is the usual pick. Where the raceway can be hit by a forklift or must carry a hazardous-location rating, you move to rigid. This guide lines the three up by NEC article so you can decide with the code in your hand, and shows how much wire space each gives you.

EMT: the indoor workhorse

Electrical metallic tubing is light, easy to cut and bends with a hand bender, which is why it dominates commercial branch-circuit work. Section 358.10 permits it exposed and concealed, in dry, damp or wet locations, and in concrete in direct contact with the earth when it is protected by approved corrosion protection. In wet locations the fittings must be listed for wet locations, and the support screws and straps must be corrosion resistant.

What EMT cannot do: it is not permitted where subject to severe physical damage, and it cannot support luminaires or other equipment (a conduit body no larger than the tubing is the exception) [358.12]. Because the tube is thin, a flattened or dented section is easy to create; the offset multiplier chart helps you bend neatly and avoid kinks.

PVC: underground, wet and corrosive

PVC does not rust, so it is the common choice for burial and for wet or corrosive spots. The trade-offs are written into Article 352:

  • Physical damage: PVC is not permitted where subject to physical damage unless identified for the application. Schedule 40 is not identified for that use; Schedule 80 is.
  • Temperature: not permitted where the ambient temperature exceeds 50 °C (122 °F) [352.12(D)], and not in plenums.
  • Support: secure within 3 ft of every box, cabinet or termination fitting, then at the intervals in Table 352.30.
  • Expansion: install expansion fittings in straight runs between fixed points where the expected length change is 1/4 in. or more [352.44]. With sun exposure, installers add 30 °F to the high ambient temperature when sizing for expansion, per the fitting maker.
  • Grounding: plastic is not a ground path, so a separate equipment grounding conductor goes in the pipe.

Rigid metal conduit: maximum protection

EC&M’s code summary notes that RMC and IMC can be used in all atmospheric conditions and occupancies, can be used where subject to physical damage (no other metal raceway can), and can go in concrete or direct burial with approved corrosion protection. They are also usable in hazardous locations, while EMT cannot. RMC can be threaded; if it is, a bushing must protect the conductors where they exit. The price is weight, labor and cost: a threaded run takes much longer to install than the same size of EMT.

The pattern: PVC Schedule 80 has the thickest wall, so it holds the least; EMT and rigid are close, with EMT slightly smaller at 3/4 in. than rigid. Check an exact size on the EMT, PVC and rigid fill charts, or run the conduit fill calculator. Our 40% rule guide explains how the limit is applied.

A quick decision guide

  1. Exposed to vehicles or impact? Use RMC or IMC (or PVC Schedule 80 where allowed).
  2. Buried or in a corrosive, wet spot? PVC is the usual first choice; RMC with corrosion protection also works.
  3. Hazardous (classified) location? Rigid or IMC, following the hazardous-location articles; not EMT, and not PVC except where specifically permitted.
  4. Indoor, dry, protected run? EMT is the standard.
  5. Short connection to vibrating equipment? Add a flexible whip for the last few feet, with a wire-type grounding conductor or a length within the 250.118 limits.

Always confirm with your local inspector: jurisdictions adopt different NEC editions and add amendments, and the permitted-use sections above are summaries, not a substitute for the code text.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use EMT underground?
EMT may be installed in concrete in direct contact with the earth when approved corrosion protection is provided [358.10(B)], but PVC is the more common choice for burial.
Does PVC conduit need a ground wire?
Yes. PVC is nonmetallic, so it cannot serve as the equipment grounding path and a separate grounding conductor is installed in the pipe.
When is Schedule 80 PVC required?
When the conduit is subject to physical damage: Schedule 40 is not identified for that use, Schedule 80 is [352.10(F)].
Can EMT be used outdoors?
Yes, in wet locations when the fittings are listed for wet locations and the support hardware is corrosion resistant [358.10, 358.42].
Which conduit holds the most wire at 3/4 trade size?
Rigid has the largest 40% area on our charts (0.220 sq in.), followed by EMT (0.213), PVC 40 (0.203) and PVC 80.
