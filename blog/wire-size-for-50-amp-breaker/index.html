Wire Size for a 50 Amp Breaker: 6 AWG or 8 AWG Copper?

Guide · Updated November 24, 2026

The short answer: a 50 amp breaker takes 6 AWG copper (or 4 AWG aluminum) in almost every home wiring job. 8 AWG copper also works, but only when every termination on the circuit is rated for 75 °C conductors. Here is why the answer splits, and how to choose.

Why there are two answers

The NEC lets you use a conductor only at the temperature column of the lowest-rated part it connects to (NEC 110.14(C)). For circuits of 100 A or less, the starting point is the 60 °C column, unless the breaker, panel and devices are listed and identified for 75 °C conductors. Look at the values around 50 A in Table 310.16:

Size60 °C column75 °C column
10 AWG copper30 A35 A
8 AWG copper40 A50 A
6 AWG copper55 A65 A
4 AWG copper70 A85 A
8 AWG aluminum35 A40 A
6 AWG aluminum40 A50 A
4 AWG aluminum55 A65 A

8 AWG copper reaches 50 A only in the 75 °C column. 6 AWG copper clears 50 A in both columns (55 A and 65 A), so it needs no terminal check. That is why electricians default to 6 AWG for 50 A circuits.

Which size for which wiring method

WiringCopperAluminum
NM cable, or any 60 °C terminal6 AWG4 AWG
Single conductors (THHN in conduit), every termination listed for 75 °C8 AWG6 AWG
Equipment ground (Table 250.122, 50 A breaker)10 AWG8 AWG

The ground size comes from NEC Table 250.122, which sizes the equipment grounding conductor from the breaker rating. A 50 A breaker falls in the row for breakers up to 60 A. If you upsize the circuit wires for distance, the ground must grow in proportion (250.122(B)).

NM cable (Romex) on a 50 A breaker

NM cable is held to the 60 °C ampacity by NEC 334.80, even though its conductors may be rated 90 °C. In the 60 °C column, 8 AWG copper carries 40 A, so a 50 A breaker needs 6/3 NM cable (55 A) for a range or RV outlet. Single THHN conductors in conduit are the only case where 8 AWG can make sense, and then only after you check the breaker and device labels.

Can 10 AWG go on a 50 A breaker?

Not on an ordinary circuit. NEC 240.4(D) limits overcurrent protection for 10 AWG copper to 30 A. Air conditioners and some motor circuits follow Article 440 instead, where the nameplate sets the breaker size, but that is a different rule for different equipment.

Does distance change the wire size?

Table 310.16 sizes a circuit for current, not length. On a long run the voltage drop matters. Using this site’s conductor resistance table (NEC Chapter 9 Table 8, uncoated), the drop of a full 50 A load over 100 ft one way on a 240 V circuit works out as:

Size (100 ft one way, 50 A, 240 V)DropPercent
6 AWG copper4.91 V2.05 %
8 AWG copper7.78 V3.24 %
4 AWG aluminum5.08 V2.12 %
6 AWG aluminum8.08 V3.37 %

The drop is 2 × length × current × resistance per 1,000 ft, divided by 1,000. Drop falls in proportion to current, so a lighter load drops less. The NEC’s informational notes suggest keeping the drop at the farthest outlet under about 3 %. At 100 ft, 6 AWG copper stays near 2 %, while 8 AWG is just over 3 %. For longer runs, go up a size and check the exact number with the voltage drop calculator.

Worked example: a garage subpanel

You are feeding a 50 A subpanel 80 ft away with THHN in conduit, and the breaker is marked for 60/75 °C wire. 8 AWG copper is allowed by the 75 °C column, but if the subpanel lugs are marked 60 °C only, you must use 6 AWG copper. Many installers choose 6 AWG anyway for the lower drop and spare capacity. Add a 10 AWG copper ground and a neutral if the subpanel serves 120 V loads. Then check the pipe: use the conduit fill calculator for the number of wires.

Common 50 A uses

  • Electric range on a 14-50R or hardwired.
  • RV outlet (14-50R) or a 40 A EV charger, which needs a breaker at 125 % of its output.
  • Hot tub, welder or a 50 A feeder to a subpanel.

Whatever the load, read the equipment nameplate and your local code edition first. See the full wire ampacity chart, the page for 6 AWG ampacity and 8 AWG ampacity, and the electrical hub.

Limits of this guide

Values assume 30 °C ambient and no more than three current-carrying conductors. Hot attics, bundled cables or more wires in one conduit need derating under NEC 310.15. This is a reference, not a substitute for the code your inspector enforces.

Frequently asked questions

What size wire for a 50 amp breaker?
6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum in NM cable or on 60 °C terminals. 8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminum works only if every termination is rated 75 °C.
Can I use 8 AWG on a 50 amp breaker?
Yes, as single conductors such as THHN in conduit when all terminals are listed for 75 °C. Not in NM cable, which is held to 40 A.
What size ground wire for 50 amps?
10 AWG copper or 8 AWG aluminum from NEC Table 250.122, larger if you upsize the circuit wires.
What wire size for a 50 amp RV outlet?
6 AWG copper for a 14-50R on a 50 A breaker, going up a size on long runs to limit voltage drop.
Can 10 AWG go on a 50 amp breaker?
Not on ordinary circuits. NEC 240.4(D) limits 10 AWG copper to a 30 A breaker.
