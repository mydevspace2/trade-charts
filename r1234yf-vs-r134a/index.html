R-1234yf vs R-134a

At 100 °F condensing, R-1234yf sits at 124.9 psig versus 124.2 psig for R-134a (+1%). At a 40 °F evaporator the pressures are 38.4 and 35.0 psig. R-1234yf is safety group A2L, R-134a is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressures are close enough that the same high-pressure gauges and hoses work, but you must switch to the matching PT scale or refrigerant setting on your digital manifold.

Glide. Both refrigerants have little or no glide.

Flammability. ASHRAE safety group A2L: lower toxicity and mildly flammable (low burning velocity). Use A2L-rated recovery machines and leak detectors, remove ignition sources, ventilate the work area, and follow the equipment manufacturer’s A2L service instructions and UL 60335-2-40 / ASHRAE 15 requirements for the installation.

Is R-1234yf a drop-in for R-134a? Treat no refrigerant as a pure drop-in. Some blends are approved retrofits, but oil, seals, metering devices, pressure controls and charge amount may change. Follow the refrigerant producer’s retrofit guidelines and the equipment manufacturer.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for A2L R-1234yf and R-134a.
  • Charge by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 1 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-1234yf run at higher pressure than R-134a?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-1234yf is about 1 psi higher (+1%) than R-134a; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by +3.4 psi.
Which is safer, R-1234yf or R-134a?
R-1234yf is ASHRAE group A2L and R-134a is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-1234yf and R-134a?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
