Conduit Fill Calculator

Mix any THHN/THWN-2 or XHHW-2 conductor sizes and counts in EMT, PVC Schedule 40/80, rigid, IMC, flex or liquid-tight. The calculator applies the NEC Chapter 9 53% / 31% / 40% limits and shows the smallest conduit that fits.

Conduit fill calculator

Rules the calculator applies

Fill limits come from Chapter 9 Table 1: one conductor 53%, two conductors 31%, three or more 40% of the raceway internal area (Table 4). Conductor areas come from Table 5 for THHN/THWN/THWN-2 and XHHW/XHHW-2. Equipment grounding conductors count. The result does not include ampacity adjustment, which you must apply separately for more than three current-carrying conductors.

Worked examples

Kitchen circuits in EMT. Two 20 A circuits sharing a raceway: four 12 AWG THHN plus one 12 AWG ground = 5 × 0.0133 = 0.0665 in². 1/2" EMT allows 0.122 in² at 40%, so 1/2" works (21.9% fill).

Subpanel feeder in PVC. Three 2 AWG THHN (0.1158 in² each) plus one 8 AWG ground (0.0366) = 0.3840 in². 1" PVC Schedule 40 allows 0.333 in², too small; 1-1/4" allows 0.581 in² and works.

Condenser whip. Two 10 AWG THHN plus a 10 AWG ground in 1/2" liquid-tight: 3 × 0.0211 = 0.0633 in², within the 40% limit for 1/2" LFMC (0.125 in²).

Choosing a raceway size in practice

The NEC limit is a maximum, not a target. Many electricians size one trade size larger than the minimum when a run has several bends, is long, or may get extra circuits later; pulling is easier and the cost difference is small for short runs. Remember that more than three current-carrying conductors in one raceway triggers the ampacity adjustment in 310.15(C)(1), which can push you to larger wire anyway.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate conduit fill?
Add the Table 5 areas of all conductors (including grounds) and compare with 40% of the conduit internal area from Table 4 when there are three or more conductors.
Can I mix wire sizes?
Yes. Add each conductor’s area; the same-size Annex C style charts only apply when all conductors are identical.
What about cables in conduit?
A multiconductor cable is treated as a single conductor using its outside diameter (Chapter 9 Note 9); an elliptical cable uses its major diameter.
