R-32 vs R-410A

At 100 °F condensing, R-32 sits at 325.7 psig versus 318.5 psig for R-410A (+2%). At a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures are 121.0 and 118.4 psig. R-32 is safety group A2L, R-410A is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressures are close enough that the same high-pressure gauges and hoses work, but you must switch to the matching PT scale or refrigerant setting on your digital manifold.

Glide. Both refrigerants have little or no glide.

Flammability. ASHRAE safety group A2L: lower toxicity and mildly flammable (low burning velocity). Use A2L-rated recovery machines and leak detectors, remove ignition sources, ventilate the work area, and follow the equipment manufacturer’s A2L service instructions and UL 60335-2-40 / ASHRAE 15 requirements for the installation.

Can you put R-32 in an R-410A system? No. A2L and other R-410A replacements are approved for new equipment designed for them, with matching compressors, oils, metering devices, leak-detection and mitigation controls. Never retrofit a system to a different refrigerant unless the equipment manufacturer specifically allows it.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for A2L R-32 and R-410A.
  • Charge blends as liquid and by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 7 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-32 run at higher pressure than R-410A?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-32 is about 7 psi higher (+2%) than R-410A; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by +2.6 psi.
Which is safer, R-32 or R-410A?
R-32 is ASHRAE group A2L and R-410A is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-32 and R-410A?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
