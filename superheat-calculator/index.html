Superheat Calculator

Enter the refrigerant, the suction pressure and the suction-line temperature. The calculator converts pressure to the saturated (dew) temperature from our verified PT data and gives superheat instantly, in °F or °C.

Calculate superheat

How superheat is calculated

Superheat = suction line temperature − saturated suction temperature. The saturated temperature comes from the PT chart at the measured suction pressure. For zeotropic blends (R-454B, R-407C, R-448A, R-449A and others) the calculator uses the dew temperature, because the refrigerant leaving the evaporator has finished boiling at the dew point.

Example with R-410A: suction pressure 118 psig is a 40 °F saturated temperature. A suction line at 52 °F gives 52 − 40 = 12 °F of superheat.

Good measurement practice

  • Let the system run 15 minutes or more with doors closed and filters clean before you judge the charge.
  • Clamp the thermometer on clean bare copper within a few inches of the pressure port and insulate it from ambient air.
  • On fixed-orifice (piston) systems compare the result with a target from indoor wet bulb and outdoor dry bulb, such as our target superheat calculator or the manufacturer’s table.
  • On TXV/EEV systems superheat is controlled by the valve; charge by subcooling unless the manufacturer says otherwise.
  • Low superheat risks liquid floodback to the compressor; high superheat suggests low charge, a restriction or low airflow on the heat-transfer side.

Frequently asked questions

What is superheat?
Superheat is how many degrees the suction vapor is above its saturation (boiling) temperature at the measured pressure: line temperature minus saturated (dew) temperature.
What is a normal superheat?
It depends on the metering device and conditions. TXV systems usually hold roughly 8–12 °F at the evaporator outlet; fixed-orifice systems need a target that changes with indoor wet bulb and outdoor temperature. Always use the manufacturer’s charging chart.
Where do I measure superheat?
Measure suction pressure and suction-line temperature at the same point, usually near the outdoor unit service valve for total superheat, with the system running at least 15 minutes.
Which column do I use for a blend like R-454B?
The dew column. Superheat is measured from the dew point.
