How to bend an offset

  1. Measure the offset depth (how far the conduit must move over).
  2. Pick the angle; multiply depth × multiplier = distance between the two marks.
  3. Shrink = depth × shrink per inch. Add it to the measurement to the obstacle so the offset ends where you need it.
  4. Make the first bend, rotate the conduit 180°, line up the second mark and bend to the same angle.

Example: 6 in offset at 22.5°: 6 × 2.613 = 15.7 in between marks (about 15-11/16 in); shrink 6 × 0.199 = 1.2 in.

Math: multiplier = csc(θ); shrink per inch = csc(θ) − cot(θ) = tan(θ/2). Field values are the rounded numbers commonly printed on bender charts. Bender take-up/gain still depends on your bender; check its manual.

Frequently asked questions

What is an offset multiplier?
The number you multiply the offset depth by to get the distance between the two bends: 1 ÷ sin(angle), the cosecant.
What is shrink?
How much shorter the conduit run gets because of the offset. Exact shrink per inch of offset = tan(angle ÷ 2); field tables round it to common fractions.
Which angle should I use?
Shallow angles (10°–22.5°) pull easier and look cleaner in tight spaces; 30° and 45° make shorter offsets. Total bends between pull points may not exceed 360°.
