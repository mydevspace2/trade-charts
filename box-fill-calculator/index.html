Pick a standard metal box or enter the marked volume, then count conductors, devices, clamps and grounds. The calculator adds the NEC 314.16(B) allowances and tells you whether the box is large enough.

The maximum-conductor columns assume conductors only, with no clamps, devices or grounds. Subtract the allowances for those first.

Counting rules in plain words

  • Each conductor that enters the box and is spliced or terminated counts once; one that passes through unbroken counts once (a loop long enough to cut counts twice).
  • Pigtails and jumpers that never leave the box do not count.
  • Each device (switch, receptacle) on a yoke counts twice, based on the largest conductor connected to it.
  • Internal cable clamps count once in total; each type of fixture stud or hickey counts once.
  • All equipment grounding conductors count once, with 1/4 extra for each one beyond four.

Source: NEC (NFPA 70) 314.16, Table 314.16(A) and 314.16(B), as reprinted in the Steel City (ABB) metallic box catalog.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate box fill?
Count conductors, then add allowances: each internal cable clamp set counts 1, each device yoke counts 2, all equipment grounding conductors together count 1 (plus 1/4 for each beyond four), each fixture stud/hickey counts 1, all based on the largest conductor (NEC 314.16(B)).
How many cubic inches per 12 AWG wire?
2.25 in³ per conductor (Table 314.16(B)). 14 AWG is 2.00 in³ and 10 AWG is 2.50 in³.
What volume does a plastic box have?
Nonmetallic boxes are marked with their volume by the manufacturer; use the marked value.
