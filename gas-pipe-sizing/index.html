Before you size

Confirm the delivery pressure with the utility or propane supplier, the allowable pressure drop your design uses, and every appliance input on its rating plate. Plan for future appliances such as a generator, pool heater or range. The tables are the code’s capacities; the installation still needs permits, pressure testing and inspection per your local code.

Read our guide: how to size gas pipe with the longest length method.

Choosing the right table

Pick the table by four things: gas (natural or propane), material (Schedule 40 steel, CSST, polyethylene for underground outdoor use), system pressure (low pressure under 2 psi, or a 2 psi / 5 psi system with a regulator at the appliances) and the design pressure drop you allow from the meter to the farthest appliance. A 0.5 in. w.c. drop is the most common low-pressure choice in residential work; a 0.3 in. drop is more conservative. Using a table with a larger drop than your design allows will undersize the pipe.

Example

A furnace (100,000 Btu/h) and water heater (40,000 Btu/h) on natural gas, black iron, low pressure, 0.5 in. w.c. drop, 40 ft to the farthest appliance: 140 CFH total. The 40 ft row of Table 402.4(2) gives 3/4 in = 170 CFH, so the main can be 3/4 in; the furnace branch (100 CFH) also needs 3/4 in and the water heater branch (40 CFH) 1/2 in (81 CFH).

Frequently asked questions

How do you size natural gas pipe?
Total the Btu/h load, convert to CFH, find the longest run, and pick the size from the matching IFGC table row with enough capacity. Repeat for each branch.
What size gas line for 100,000 Btu?
It depends on length and material. In Schedule 40 iron at under 2 psi with 0.5 in. w.c. drop, 100 CFH needs 1/2" up to about 20 ft and 3/4" up to about 70 ft (Table 402.4(2)). Use the calculator for your run.
Is propane sized differently?
Yes. Propane has higher energy per cubic foot, so IFGC propane tables are in thousands of Btu/h and allow smaller pipe for the same load.
