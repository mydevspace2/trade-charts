R-407F PT Chart

At 40 °F, R-407F has a dew pressure of 73.3 psig and a bubble pressure of 90.3 psig (temperature glide about 9.7 °F). At 100 °F the bubble (liquid) pressure is 249.3 psig. Use the lookup below for any temperature or pressure, or scroll to the full chart in °F and °C.

How to read this R-407F PT chart

R-407F is a zeotropic blend, so it does not boil at a single temperature for a given pressure. The bubble point is where the liquid starts to boil; the dew point is where the last drop of liquid has evaporated. The difference is the temperature glide. Inside the evaporator the refrigerant moves from bubble toward dew, so when you calculate superheat use the dew column; in the condenser it moves from dew to bubble, so for subcooling use the bubble column. Using the wrong column on a high-glide blend can throw your superheat or subcooling off by several degrees and lead to an over- or under-charge.

All pressures are gauge pressures at sea level (psig = psia − 14.7). At high altitude a gauge zeroed to the local atmosphere reads higher than the chart for the same temperature (roughly 0.5 psi per 1,000 ft); digital manifolds with an altitude setting correct for this. Values shown in inHg vac are vacuum (below atmospheric pressure). The chart describes saturated refrigerant only; it does not tell you whether a system is correctly charged by itself.

Worked example: superheat and subcooling with R-407F

Superheat. Suction pressure at the service valve reads 73 psig. On this chart that pressure is a dew temperature of about 40 °F. Your clamp thermometer on the suction line near the same point reads 52 °F, so superheat = 52 − 40 = 12 °F. Compare it with the target from the manufacturer’s charging chart or our target superheat calculator (fixed orifice systems).

Subcooling. Liquid line pressure reads 268 psig, which is a bubble temperature of about 105 °F. The liquid line measures 95 °F, so subcooling = 105 − 95 = 10 °F. TXV and EEV systems are normally charged to the subcooling printed on the outdoor unit’s rating plate. Do the math automatically with the superheat and subcooling calculators.

How this chart was made and checked

Pressures are computed with CoolProp 8, an open-source property library that implements the same Helmholtz equations of state used by NIST REFPROP, at 1 °F steps with the ASHRAE Standard 34 blend composition. We then checked the result against published charts: Honeywell pressure-based PT chart (93 points, max 0.05 °F); Hudson Technologies PT chart (liquid) (32 points, max 0.04 °F); ASHRAE 34 bubble/dew at 1 atm (2 points, max 0.11 °F). Across all 127 compared points the largest difference is 0.11 °F in saturation temperature (our pass limit is 1.0 °F, about the resolution of a good digital gauge). Points within 2 psi of atmospheric pressure are not compared because printed charts switch to vacuum units there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the R-407F pressure at 40 °F?
R-407F saturates at 73.3 psig (dew) and 90.3 psig (bubble) at 40 °F.
What is the R-407F pressure at 100 °F?
The bubble (liquid) saturation pressure of R-407F at 100 °F is 249.3 psig and the dew pressure is 220.6 psig.
Should I use bubble or dew for R-407F?
Use the dew point for superheat (evaporator and suction line) and the bubble point for subcooling (condenser and liquid line). For R-407F the glide at 40 °F is about 9.7 °F.
Can I top off a leaking R-407F system?
R-407F is a blend; a vapor leak can shift its composition. Manufacturers recommend charging blends as liquid and, after a large leak, recovering and recharging with new refrigerant. Follow the equipment manufacturer’s instructions.
Is R-407F flammable?
No. R-407F is ASHRAE safety group A1 (no flame propagation). It can still displace oxygen and form toxic products in a flame.
Where does this R-407F chart come from?
CoolProp 8, an open-source REFPROP-equivalent property library, checked against 127 points from published charts with a maximum difference of 0.11 °F. Sources are listed on the page.
