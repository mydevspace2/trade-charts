R-22 vs R-410A

At 100 °F condensing, R-22 sits at 195.9 psig versus 318.5 psig for R-410A (-38%). At a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures are 68.6 and 118.4 psig. R-22 is safety group A1, R-410A is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressure levels are clearly different, so use gauges, hoses and recovery cylinders rated for the higher-pressure refrigerant and select the right refrigerant on your manifold.

Glide. Both refrigerants have little or no glide.

Flammability. Both are A1 (no flame propagation), so no flammable-refrigerant procedures are needed, but standard refrigerant safety still applies.

Can R-410A go into an R-22 system? No. R-410A runs at roughly 60% higher pressures and uses POE oil; R-22 equipment is not rated for it. R-22 systems are serviced with reclaimed R-22 or an approved retrofit blend such as R-407C, R-422D, R-427A or R-438A, following the blend maker’s retrofit guide.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for R-22 and R-410A.
  • Charge blends as liquid and by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 123 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-22 run at higher pressure than R-410A?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-22 is about 123 psi lower (-38%) than R-410A; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by -49.8 psi.
Which is safer, R-22 or R-410A?
R-22 is ASHRAE group A1 and R-410A is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-22 and R-410A?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
