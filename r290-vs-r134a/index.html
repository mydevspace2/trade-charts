R-290 vs R-134a

At 100 °F condensing, R-290 sits at 173.9 psig versus 124.2 psig for R-134a (+40%). At a 40 °F evaporator the pressures are 63.9 and 35.0 psig. R-290 is safety group A3, R-134a is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressure levels are clearly different, so use gauges, hoses and recovery cylinders rated for the higher-pressure refrigerant and select the right refrigerant on your manifold.

Glide. Both refrigerants have little or no glide.

Flammability. ASHRAE safety group A3: lower toxicity and highly flammable. Only technicians trained for hydrocarbon refrigerants should service it, with spark-proof tools, a hydrocarbon-rated leak detector and recovery equipment, and no ignition sources. Never substitute it into equipment not designed for it.

Is R-290 a drop-in for R-134a? Treat no refrigerant as a pure drop-in. Some blends are approved retrofits, but oil, seals, metering devices, pressure controls and charge amount may change. Follow the refrigerant producer’s retrofit guidelines and the equipment manufacturer.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for R-290 and R-134a.
  • Charge by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 50 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-290 run at higher pressure than R-134a?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-290 is about 50 psi higher (+40%) than R-134a; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by +28.9 psi.
Which is safer, R-290 or R-134a?
R-290 is ASHRAE group A3 and R-134a is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-290 and R-134a?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
