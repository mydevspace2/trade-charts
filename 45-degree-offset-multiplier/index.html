45° Offset Multiplier

The multiplier for a 45-degree offset is 1.414 (commonly rounded to 1.4 (some use 1.41)). Shrink is 0.414 in per inch of offset depth (3/8 in on most bender charts).

Using a 45° offset

Distance between bends = offset depth × 1.414. For a 4 in offset that is 5.66 in. Add the shrink (1.66 in for 4 in) to your measurement to the obstruction. A 45° offset is compact but harder to pull through; count it as 90° of the 360° allowed between pull points.

Worked examples for a 45° offset

Example 1: a 4 in. offset. Distance between the two bend marks = 4 × 1.414 = 5.66 in. The run gets shorter by 4 × 0.414 = 1.66 in. (exact; bender charts round the per-inch value). If the conduit must end exactly at a box, measure to the box, then add that 1.66 in. of shrink to the first mark so the offset lands where you want it.

Example 2: a 7.5 in. offset. Marks are 7.5 × 1.414 = 10.61 in. apart, and shrink is 3.11 in. Notice that the spacing grows in direct proportion to the depth, so a deeper offset needs a longer clear run between the obstruction and the next fitting.

How many 45° offsets fit before the 360° limit

NEC rules for metal and nonmetallic conduit limit the total bends between pull points (conduit bodies, boxes or enclosures) to 360 degrees, including offsets. A standard offset is two bends of 45°, so it uses 90° of that allowance. That means 4 45° offsets would use 360° of the 360°, and nothing is left for a 90° stub-up. Plan the 90° bends first, then see how many offsets still fit.

Marking and bending steps

  1. Measure how far the conduit must move over (the offset depth) and mark the first bend position on the conduit.
  2. Calculate the spacing: depth × 1.414. Measure that distance from the first mark and make the second mark.
  3. Bend the first mark to 45° with the bender’s arrow or 45° mark aligned to the first bend mark, as your bender shows.
  4. Rotate the conduit 180° (flip it so the first bend faces the opposite direction) and keep the bends in the same plane, then bend the second mark to 45°.
  5. Lay the conduit on a flat floor to check that both legs are in one plane and the offset depth matches your measurement.

Choosing between angles

The multiplier is simply 1 ÷ sin(45°). A larger multiplier means the bends are farther apart and the conduit moves over more gradually, which is easier on a long pull; a smaller one gives a short, abrupt offset that fits in tight spots. Compare the 10°, 30° and other angles on the offset multiplier chart, and use the EMT fill chart to confirm the pipe you are bending is sized for the wire.

Frequently asked questions

What is the multiplier for a 45 degree offset?
1.414 exact (1 ÷ sin 45°); bending charts round it to 1.4 (some use 1.41).
What is the shrink for a 45 degree offset?
0.414 in per inch of offset depth (field value 3/8 in per inch).
