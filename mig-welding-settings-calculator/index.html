MIG Welder Settings Calculator

Enter material thickness and wire diameter for a starting amperage and wire feed speed on mild steel, using Miller Electric’s published rules of thumb. Then fine-tune voltage on scrap with your machine’s door chart.

Starting settings

The three rules

  1. Amperage from thickness: about 1 amp for every 0.001 in of material (1/8 in = 125 A).
  2. Wire size from amperage: .023 in 30–130 A; .030 in 40–145 A; .035 in 50–180 A; .045 in 75–250 A.
  3. Wire feed speed from amperage: multiply amps by 3.5 (.023), 2 (.030), 1.6 (.035) or 1 (.045) inches per minute per amp.

Fine-tuning on scrap

  • Run a bead on the same thickness and joint type. A steady “bacon frying” sound in short-circuit transfer means you are close.
  • Too much spatter and a narrow, ropey bead: raise voltage slightly or lower wire speed.
  • Wide, flat bead with poor penetration or burn-through: lower voltage or travel faster.
  • Change one setting at a time and write down what works; next time start there.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps for 1/8 inch steel MIG?
About 125 A by Miller’s rule of 1 amp per 0.001 in of thickness. With .030 wire that is about 250 ipm wire speed, with .035 about 200 ipm.
What wire size should I use?
Miller recommends .023 in for 30–130 A, .030 in for 40–145 A, .035 in for 50–180 A and .045 in for 75–250 A. Choose the wire that covers your most common thickness.
Is this better than my door chart?
No. The chart inside your welder’s door is written for that machine and should be your first reference. Use these rules when you have no chart, then fine-tune on scrap.
