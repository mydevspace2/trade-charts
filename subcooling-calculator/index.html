Subcooling Calculator

Enter the refrigerant, liquid-line pressure and liquid-line temperature. The calculator finds the saturated (bubble) temperature from verified PT data and returns the subcooling.

Calculate subcooling

How subcooling is calculated

Subcooling = saturated condensing temperature − liquid line temperature. The saturated condensing temperature is read from the PT chart at the measured liquid pressure. For blends the calculator uses the bubble temperature.

Example with R-410A: liquid pressure 340 psig is about 105 °F saturated. A liquid line at 95 °F gives 105 − 95 = 10 °F of subcooling.

Reading the result

  • Below target: often low charge, but also check for a dirty condenser coil, a restriction or a TXV that is overfeeding.
  • Above target: often overcharge, or a restriction upstream of the metering device holding liquid back in the condenser.
  • Always confirm with superheat and airflow before adding or removing refrigerant, and weigh the charge when the manufacturer gives a line-length adjustment.

Good measurement practice

  • Measure at the liquid service valve with the system stable for at least 15 minutes and the outdoor coil clean.
  • Use an insulated pipe clamp on clean copper; a reading taken in direct sun or on a dirty line will be off.
  • On heat pumps in heating mode, follow the manufacturer: many specify weighing in the charge rather than charging by subcooling.
  • For long line sets, add or remove refrigerant per the installation manual’s per-foot adjustment.

Subcooling on TXV vs fixed-orifice systems

On a TXV or EEV system the valve keeps evaporator superheat in a narrow band, so extra refrigerant has nowhere to go except the condenser, where it backs up as subcooled liquid. That makes subcooling the most sensitive indicator of charge on those systems, and it is why outdoor units list a target subcooling (often with a tolerance such as ±3 °F). On fixed-orifice systems subcooling still tells you about the condenser, but the charge is set by superheat; use the target superheat calculator.

Worked example with a blend

An R-454B system shows 300 psig at the liquid valve. The bubble temperature at 300 psig is about 99 °F (see the R-454B PT chart). The liquid line measures 89 °F, so subcooling is about 10 °F. Using the dew column by mistake would give a slightly higher saturation temperature and over-state subcooling by the glide, which for some blends is several degrees.

Frequently asked questions

What is subcooling?
Subcooling is how many degrees the liquid refrigerant is below its saturation (bubble) temperature: saturated liquid temperature minus liquid-line temperature.
What should subcooling be?
Use the target printed on the outdoor unit or in the installation manual. Many residential TXV systems specify roughly 8–12 °F, but the rating plate value rules.
Which column do I use for a blend?
The bubble column: subcooling is measured from the bubble point, where the refrigerant is fully condensed.
Where do I measure subcooling?
Take liquid-line pressure and temperature at the same point, usually at the liquid service valve of the outdoor unit.
