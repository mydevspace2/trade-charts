R-449A vs R-448A

At 100 °F condensing, R-449A sits at 240.7 psig versus 244.2 psig for R-448A (-1%). At a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures are 71.2 and 71.2 psig. R-449A is safety group A1, R-448A is A1.

What changes in the field

Gauges and hoses. The pressures are close enough that the same high-pressure gauges and hoses work, but you must switch to the matching PT scale or refrigerant setting on your digital manifold.

Glide. At least one of these is a zeotropic blend with measurable glide: read superheat from the dew column and subcooling from the bubble column, and charge blends as liquid.

Flammability. Both are A1 (no flame propagation), so no flammable-refrigerant procedures are needed, but standard refrigerant safety still applies.

Is R-449A a drop-in for R-448A? Treat no refrigerant as a pure drop-in. Some blends are approved retrofits, but oil, seals, metering devices, pressure controls and charge amount may change. Follow the refrigerant producer’s retrofit guidelines and the equipment manufacturer.

Service checklist when you work on both

  • Confirm the refrigerant on the rating plate before connecting gauges; never rely on cylinder color or memory.
  • Use separate recovery cylinders for each refrigerant and label them; mixed refrigerant cannot be reclaimed normally.
  • Check that your recovery machine, vacuum pump and leak detector are rated for R-449A and R-448A.
  • Charge blends as liquid and by weight to the nameplate charge, then confirm with superheat or subcooling.
  • Read pressures against the right PT chart; at 100 °F condensing the two differ by 3 psi.

Frequently asked questions

Does R-449A run at higher pressure than R-448A?
At a 100 °F condensing temperature R-449A is about 3 psi lower (-1%) than R-448A; at a 40 °F evaporator the dew pressures differ by -0.0 psi.
Which is safer, R-449A or R-448A?
R-449A is ASHRAE group A1 and R-448A is group A1. A1 = no flame propagation, A2L = mildly flammable, A3 = highly flammable, B = higher toxicity.
Can I mix R-449A and R-448A?
No. Never mix refrigerants. Mixed refrigerant cannot be charged correctly or reclaimed normally and must be recovered as contaminated refrigerant.
