1-1/4" EMT Conduit Fill

A 1-1/4-inch EMT holds up to 45 × 12 AWG or 28 × 10 AWG THHN/THWN-2 conductors at the NEC 40% fill limit. Its internal area is 1.496 in² (40% = 0.598 in²).

Mixed sizes example

Three 10 AWG THHN plus one 12 AWG THHN ground: 3 × 0.0211 + 0.0133 = 0.0766 in², which is 5.1% of 1-1/4" EMT (within the 40% limit). For mixed sizes always add the areas instead of using the same-size table.

Notes

  • Counts are for conductors only. Grounding conductors count toward fill.
  • With more than three current-carrying conductors, apply ampacity adjustment (NEC 310.15(C)(1)).
  • A nipple of 24 in. or less may be filled to 60%.

How many wires fit in 1-1/4 inch EMT?

NEC Chapter 9, Table 4 gives 1-1/4 inch EMT a total internal area of 1.496 sq in. For three or more conductors Table 1 allows 40% of that, 0.598 sq in. Dividing by the conductor area from Table 5 (THHN/THWN-2) gives the maximum number of identical conductors:

Worked example

A branch circuit needs three 12 AWG THHN conductors plus a 12 AWG equipment grounding conductor, four conductors in all. Total area is 4 × 0.0133 = 0.0532 sq in. The 40% allowance of 1-1/4 inch EMT is 0.598 sq in, so it fits (4% of the conduit). The table above says up to 44 12 AWG conductors fit.

How to use this page

  1. List every conductor, including equipment grounds (NEC Chapter 9, Note 3).
  2. Look up each insulated conductor area in Table 5; bare grounds use Table 8.
  3. Add the areas and compare the total with the 40% column.
  4. If it is over, step up one trade size and recheck. Use the conduit fill calculator for mixed sizes.

Rules that change the answer: a section of raceway 24 inches or shorter may be filled to 60% (Note 4), and different insulation types have different areas, so XHHW and THHN results differ. Read the NEC 40% rule explained for the notes in full, and always check with your local inspector, because local amendments exist. Conductor derating for more than three current-carrying conductors is a separate check on the electrical hub.
