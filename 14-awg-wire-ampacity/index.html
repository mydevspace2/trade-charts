14 AWG Wire Ampacity

14 AWG copper is rated 15 A at 60 °C, 20 A at 75 °C and 25 A at 90 °C in NEC Table 310.16. These are base values for not more than three current-carrying conductors at 30 °C (86 °F) ambient.

14 AWG ampacity (NEC Table 310.16)

Conductor60 °C (TW, UF)75 °C (THWN, XHHW, THW)90 °C (THHN, THWN-2, XHHW-2)
Copper15 A20 A25 A

Small-conductor rule. Unless specifically permitted elsewhere in the Code, the overcurrent device protecting 14 AWG copper may not exceed 15 A (NEC 240.4(D)), even though the table shows a higher ampacity.

Picking the right column

A conductor’s usable ampacity is limited by the lowest-rated part of the circuit. Most breakers, panelboards and device terminals are rated 60 °C or 75 °C (NEC 110.14(C)), so a THHN conductor with a 90 °C insulation rating is usually sized from the 60 °C or 75 °C column. The 90 °C value is still useful: you may apply ambient-temperature correction and bundling adjustment factors to the 90 °C ampacity, as long as the final result does not exceed the terminal-rated column.

When the base ampacity changes

  • Hot locations (attics, rooftops, boiler rooms): apply the ambient correction factors of 310.15(B).
  • More than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway or cable: apply 310.15(C)(1) adjustment (for example 80% for 4–6 conductors).
  • Continuous loads: size the conductor and breaker for 125% of the continuous load (210.19, 215.2).

14 AWG copper after bundling adjustment

NEC 310.15(C)(1) reduces ampacity when a raceway or cable holds more than three current-carrying conductors. Applied to the 90 °C value of 25 A (THHN/THWN-2), then limited to the 75 °C terminal value of 20 A:

Ambient temperature correction (310.15(B)) applies on top of this when the conductors run through spaces hotter than 30 °C (86 °F).

Voltage drop check for 14 AWG copper

Table 8 gives 14 AWG stranded copper a DC resistance of 3.14 Ω per 1,000 ft at 75 °C. At 16 A on a 240 V single-phase circuit, a 100 ft one-way run drops about 10.0 V (4.2%). Check long runs with the voltage drop calculator.

Worked example: bundling and terminals for 14 AWG copper

14 AWG copper THHN starts at 25 A in the 90 °C column. With six current-carrying conductors in one conduit the 80% factor of Table 310.15(C)(1) gives 25 × 0.80 = 20.0 A. With nine conductors (70%) it is 17.5 A, and with twelve (50%) it is 12.5 A. The final ampacity can never exceed the termination column, which is 20 A at 75 °C and 15 A at 60 °C, so use the lower of the adjusted value and the terminal value. Our derating guide works through the full method, and the column guide explains why the terminals set the limit.

Checklist before you pick 14 AWG

  1. Read the temperature marking on the breaker and the lugs.
  2. Count current-carrying conductors in the raceway; grounds do not count.
  3. Apply ambient and bundling adjustments from the 90 °C value if the insulation allows.
  4. Check voltage drop with the voltage drop calculator on long runs.
  5. Confirm the conduit size on the conduit fill calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps can 14 AWG copper carry?
Per NEC Table 310.16: 15 A (60 °C), 20 A (75 °C), 25 A (90 °C), before any correction or adjustment. Overcurrent protection for 14 AWG copper is limited to 15 A by NEC 240.4(D) in most cases.
Which column do I use for 14 AWG?
Use the column matching the lowest temperature rating among the wire, the terminals and the device. Equipment 100 A or less (or 14–1 AWG) is generally 60 °C unless marked 75 °C (NEC 110.14(C)). The 90 °C column is a starting point for derating only.
