How to use a PT chart

Read the pressure on your gauge, find it on the chart for the refrigerant in the system, and read the saturated temperature. On the suction side subtract that temperature from the measured suction-line temperature to get superheat; on the liquid side subtract the measured liquid-line temperature from the saturated temperature to get subcooling. For zeotropic blends use the dew column for superheat and the bubble column for subcooling. Our step-by-step guide walks through both with numbers.

All charts on this site share the same layout, units toggle and print style, so a printed set makes a compact truck reference. Pressures are sea-level gauge values.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PT chart?
A pressure-temperature (PT) chart lists the saturation pressure of a refrigerant at each temperature. Technicians use it to convert gauge pressure into the saturated temperature needed for superheat and subcooling.
Why do some charts have two pressure columns?
Zeotropic blends such as R-454B, R-407C and R-448A boil over a temperature range (glide). The bubble column is used for subcooling and the dew column for superheat.
Are these PT charts accurate?
Each chart is computed with a REFPROP-equivalent equation of state (or taken from the manufacturer chart for R-438A) and checked against published manufacturer and NIST data; every chart page shows how many points were compared and the largest difference.
Are the pressures gauge or absolute?
Gauge pressure at sea level (psig). Vacuum values are shown in inches of mercury.
