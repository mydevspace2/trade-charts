Voltage Drop Calculator

Enter wire size and material, one-way length, load current and system voltage. The calculator uses NEC Chapter 9 Table 8 conductor resistance and also suggests the smallest size that keeps drop under 3%.

Calculate voltage drop

The formula

VD = (2 or 1.732) × L × R × I ÷ 1000

Use 2 for single-phase and DC circuits (current flows out and back) and 1.732 (√3) for balanced three-phase circuits. L is the one-way length in feet, I the load in amperes and R the resistance in ohms per 1,000 ft. Example: 10 AWG copper (1.24 Ω/kft), 100 ft, 24 A, 240 V single-phase: 2 × 100 × 1.24 × 24 ÷ 1000 = 5.95 V, or 2.5%.

Why voltage drop matters

Motors and compressors run hotter and draw more current on low voltage, electronic loads can drop out, and lighting dims. Keeping drop near 3% on branch circuits and 5% overall is the NEC recommendation; equipment manufacturers may require tighter limits. Long runs to detached garages, wells, EV chargers, condensers and solar arrays are the usual problem cases.

Quick reference: 3% maximum one-way length

For 240 V single-phase copper at 80% of the 75 °C rating (a common continuous-load case), using Table 8 resistance: 12 AWG at 16 A reaches 3% at about 114 ft; 10 AWG at 24 A at about 121 ft; 8 AWG at 40 A at about 116 ft; 6 AWG at 52 A at about 140 ft. Longer than that, upsize or accept more drop where the equipment allows it. Check your own numbers above; these are examples, not limits.

Frequently asked questions

What voltage drop is acceptable?
NEC informational notes recommend no more than 3% on a branch circuit and 5% total for feeder plus branch circuit. They are recommendations, not requirements, except where another rule (for example fire pumps) applies.
Which formula does the calculator use?
Single-phase: VD = 2 × L × R × I ÷ 1000. Three-phase: VD = 1.732 × L × R × I ÷ 1000, with L the one-way length in feet and R the NEC Chapter 9 Table 8 resistance in ohms per 1,000 ft.
Is Table 8 resistance accurate for AC?
It is DC resistance at 75 °C. For small conductors it is very close to AC impedance; for large conductors (1/0 and up) in steel conduit, Table 9 effective Z gives a more accurate AC result.
