TIG Welding Amperage Chart

Starting TIG amperage by steel thickness and the current range of each tungsten diameter. Use a foot pedal or remote to fine-tune from these numbers.

The thickness rule here is the same 1 amp per 0.001 in guideline Miller publishes for MIG, widely used as a TIG starting point on steel. Stainless usually needs a little less current; aluminum (AC) needs more and depends heavily on balance and frequency settings, so follow your machine’s guide.

Tips

  • Grind the tungsten lengthwise to a point for DC; the included angle changes the arc shape.
  • Typical argon flow is 15–25 cfh; more is not better and can pull in air.
  • Use filler rod no thicker than the base metal for thin sheet.

Setting up the machine

Set the maximum amperage on the machine about 10–20% above the starting value in the chart and control the actual current with the pedal; this gives room to push through a heat sink without changing settings. For DC steel and stainless, use DCEN (electrode negative). Pick the tungsten diameter whose current range covers the amperage you need, and grind it to a point lengthwise. Keep the cup size and gas flow appropriate to the joint access; a gas lens helps on outside corners and long stick-out.

For MIG settings see the MIG settings calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps to TIG weld 1/8 inch steel?
A common starting point is about 1 amp per 0.001 in of thickness, so roughly 125 A for 1/8 in mild steel, adjusted with the foot pedal.
Which tungsten size for 150 amps?
Per Miller’s table, 3/32 in 2% tungsten covers about 125–200 A; 1/8 in covers about 150–325 A.
