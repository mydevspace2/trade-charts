How to find and read the serial number

  1. Find the data plate. On a tank water heater it is a sticker on the upper side of the jacket, usually facing the front. On an outdoor AC condenser it is on the side panel near the service valves; on a furnace or air handler it is inside the door or on the cabinet side.
  2. Copy the serial number exactly, including letters and any leading zeros. Photograph the plate so you can zoom in on worn characters.
  3. Note the brand and model. Many brands share a factory or a parent company, so the maker on the label decides which decoder applies.
  4. Pick the brand page below and enter the serial. The decoder shows the manufacture date and the format rule it used.
  5. Treat the result as the manufacture date. The unit may have been installed months later, so check the install paperwork or warranty registration if the exact install date matters.

Why a decoder can say the format is unknown

Manufacturers change serial formats when they change plants or ownership, and some brands do not publish a residential format at all. When a serial does not match a published rule, the page says so instead of guessing. In that case call the manufacturer with the model and serial, or check for a date stamp on the data plate, which is often printed in a separate field.

What age tells you, and what it does not

Age helps you plan repair-or-replace decisions, warranty checks and insurance paperwork, but condition matters as much as years. A well-maintained unit can outlast a neglected newer one. Use age alongside the symptoms, repair cost and efficiency. For deeper reading see how to tell the age of a water heater and how old is my AC unit.
