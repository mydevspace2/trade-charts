R-438A PT Chart

At 40 °F, R-438A has a dew pressure of 60.5 psig and a bubble pressure of 74.5 psig (temperature glide about 9.4 °F). At 100 °F the bubble (liquid) pressure is 210.6 psig. Use the lookup below for any temperature or pressure, or scroll to the full chart in °F and °C.

How to read this R-438A PT chart

R-438A is a zeotropic blend, so it does not boil at a single temperature for a given pressure. The bubble point is where the liquid starts to boil; the dew point is where the last drop of liquid has evaporated. The difference is the temperature glide. Inside the evaporator the refrigerant moves from bubble toward dew, so when you calculate superheat use the dew column; in the condenser it moves from dew to bubble, so for subcooling use the bubble column. Using the wrong column on a high-glide blend can throw your superheat or subcooling off by several degrees and lead to an over- or under-charge.

All pressures are gauge pressures at sea level (psig = psia − 14.7). At high altitude a gauge zeroed to the local atmosphere reads higher than the chart for the same temperature (roughly 0.5 psi per 1,000 ft); digital manifolds with an altitude setting correct for this. Values shown in inHg vac are vacuum (below atmospheric pressure). The chart describes saturated refrigerant only; it does not tell you whether a system is correctly charged by itself.

Worked example: superheat and subcooling with R-438A

Superheat. Suction pressure at the service valve reads 61 psig. On this chart that pressure is a dew temperature of about 40 °F. Your clamp thermometer on the suction line near the same point reads 52 °F, so superheat = 52 − 40 = 12 °F. Compare it with the target from the manufacturer’s charging chart or our target superheat calculator (fixed orifice systems).

Subcooling. Liquid line pressure reads 226 psig, which is a bubble temperature of about 105 °F. The liquid line measures 95 °F, so subcooling = 105 − 95 = 10 °F. TXV and EEV systems are normally charged to the subcooling printed on the outdoor unit’s rating plate. Do the math automatically with the superheat and subcooling calculators.

How this chart was made and checked

This table is taken directly from the DuPont/Chemours ISCEON® MO99™ (R-438A) pressure-temperature chart, which lists bubble and dew temperatures for each gauge pressure. We converted it to a temperature-based layout by linear interpolation between the published pressure rows (the open CoolProp library cannot model this five-component blend reliably, so we did not compute it). We then checked the result against published charts: Hudson Technologies PT chart (liquid) (34 points, max 0.10 °F). Across all 34 compared points the largest difference is 0.10 °F in saturation temperature (our pass limit is 1.0 °F, about the resolution of a good digital gauge). Points within 2 psi of atmospheric pressure are not compared because printed charts switch to vacuum units there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the R-438A pressure at 40 °F?
R-438A saturates at 60.5 psig (dew) and 74.5 psig (bubble) at 40 °F.
What is the R-438A pressure at 100 °F?
The bubble (liquid) saturation pressure of R-438A at 100 °F is 210.6 psig and the dew pressure is 187.3 psig.
Should I use bubble or dew for R-438A?
Use the dew point for superheat (evaporator and suction line) and the bubble point for subcooling (condenser and liquid line). For R-438A the glide at 40 °F is about 9.4 °F.
Can I top off a leaking R-438A system?
R-438A is a blend; a vapor leak can shift its composition. Manufacturers recommend charging blends as liquid and, after a large leak, recovering and recharging with new refrigerant. Follow the equipment manufacturer’s instructions.
Is R-438A flammable?
No. R-438A is ASHRAE safety group A1 (no flame propagation). It can still displace oxygen and form toxic products in a flame.
Where does this R-438A chart come from?
The DuPont/Chemours MO99 chart, checked against 34 points from published charts with a maximum difference of 0.10 °F. Sources are listed on the page.
