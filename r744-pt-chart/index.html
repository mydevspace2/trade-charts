How to read this R-744 PT chart

R-744 is a single-component refrigerant, so each temperature has exactly one saturation pressure. Find the measured pressure in the psig column and read the saturated temperature beside it (or type it into the lookup above). That saturated temperature is the starting point for both superheat (suction side) and subcooling (liquid side).

All pressures are gauge pressures at sea level (psig = psia − 14.7). At high altitude a gauge zeroed to the local atmosphere reads higher than the chart for the same temperature (roughly 0.5 psi per 1,000 ft); digital manifolds with an altitude setting correct for this. Values shown in inHg vac are vacuum (below atmospheric pressure). The chart describes saturated refrigerant only; it does not tell you whether a system is correctly charged by itself.

How this chart was made and checked

Pressures are computed with CoolProp 8, an open-source property library that implements the same Helmholtz equations of state used by NIST REFPROP, at 1 °F steps. We then checked the result against published charts: Hudson Technologies PT chart (26 points, max 0.03 °F); NIST Chemistry WebBook saturation data (81 points, max 0.00 °F). Across all 107 compared points the largest difference is 0.03 °F in saturation temperature (our pass limit is 1.0 °F, about the resolution of a good digital gauge). Points within 2 psi of atmospheric pressure are not compared because printed charts switch to vacuum units there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the R-744 pressure at 40 °F?
R-744 saturates at 552.9 psig at 40 °F.
Is R-744 flammable?
No. R-744 is ASHRAE safety group A1 (no flame propagation). It can still displace oxygen and form toxic products in a flame.
