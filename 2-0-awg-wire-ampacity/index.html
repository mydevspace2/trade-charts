2/0 AWG Wire Ampacity

2/0 AWG copper is rated 145 A at 60 °C, 175 A at 75 °C and 195 A at 90 °C in NEC Table 310.16; aluminum is 115, 135 and 150 A. These are base values for not more than three current-carrying conductors at 30 °C (86 °F) ambient.

Picking the right column

A conductor’s usable ampacity is limited by the lowest-rated part of the circuit. Most breakers, panelboards and device terminals are rated 60 °C or 75 °C (NEC 110.14(C)), so a THHN conductor with a 90 °C insulation rating is usually sized from the 60 °C or 75 °C column. The 90 °C value is still useful: you may apply ambient-temperature correction and bundling adjustment factors to the 90 °C ampacity, as long as the final result does not exceed the terminal-rated column.

When the base ampacity changes

  • Hot locations (attics, rooftops, boiler rooms): apply the ambient correction factors of 310.15(B).
  • More than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway or cable: apply 310.15(C)(1) adjustment (for example 80% for 4–6 conductors).
  • Continuous loads: size the conductor and breaker for 125% of the continuous load (210.19, 215.2).

2/0 AWG copper after bundling adjustment

NEC 310.15(C)(1) reduces ampacity when a raceway or cable holds more than three current-carrying conductors. Applied to the 90 °C value of 195 A (THHN/THWN-2), then limited to the 75 °C terminal value of 175 A:

Voltage drop check for 2/0 AWG copper

Table 8 gives 2/0 AWG stranded copper a DC resistance of 0.0967 Ω per 1,000 ft at 75 °C. At 140 A on a 240 V single-phase circuit, a 100 ft one-way run drops about 2.7 V (1.1%). Check long runs with the voltage drop calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps can 2/0 AWG copper carry?
Per NEC Table 310.16: 145 A (60 °C), 175 A (75 °C), 195 A (90 °C), before any correction or adjustment.
Which column do I use for 2/0 AWG?
Use the column matching the lowest temperature rating among the wire, the terminals and the device. Equipment 100 A or less (or 14–1 AWG) is generally 60 °C unless marked 75 °C (NEC 110.14(C)). The 90 °C column is a starting point for derating only.
How many amps can 2/0 AWG aluminum carry?
115 A (60 °C), 135 A (75 °C), 150 A (90 °C) per NEC Table 310.16.
